### ch_09.001
## IX. Stemmons, Again

### ch_09.002
The valet brings the car. The night hasn't cooled off at all. You get in, and the screen offers you what it always offers: HOME, the blue ribbon, the gray, smooth world, the car's dozen careful futures per second. You leave it there [ambiguous: "lo" = what it offers / the screen's offer]. You put both hands on the wheel, and you drive.

### ch_09.003
You also lower the window. The air comes in warm, the pavement still giving back the heat of the day, and with it come in the sound of the tires, and the hum of the highway, and somewhere a siren that is not for you.

### ch_09.004
The Stemmons, north. The same warehouses, the same levee, the same dark floodway [lit. relief channel] on your left, and the sphere of the Reunion tower in the mirror, getting smaller, still shining. You pass the airport exit and this time you let it go. On the right, a 737 comes down over Lake Bachman with everything lit, so low and so close that you can read the airline on its tail, the last one arriving from somewhere.

### ch_09.005
The dashboard says 11:31. In London it is 4:31, almost morning, and she is asleep, or close, somewhere in the tower that lies behind you, five hours ahead. On Sunday your clocks go back, and London will again be six hours away, where it belongs, and by then she will already be at home. None of that is sad, exactly. It is only true.

### ch_09.006
Very far away, to the northwest, higher and slower than the Love Field traffic, a light hangs in the darkness, sinking toward the airport. Then another one behind it, and another: the same patient row that you made out from floor forty-one at seven without once wondering who was in them [lit. who was going in them; "ellas" = the lights]. Now you wonder it. Someone on that airplane is being waited for [lit. they are waiting for him]. Someone in front of those frosted doors holds up a cell phone with a name, their arm gets tired, they switch arms. And someone on that airplane is going to come out through those doors with no one to wait for them [lit. him], telling themselves [lit. himself] that it is fine [ambiguous: "que está bien" = that it is fine / that he is fine]. It is going to be fine [ambiguous: "Va a estar bien" = it / he is going to be fine]. You hope that someone holds up a sign anyway.

### ch_09.007
Tomorrow, if someone asks you what you did tonight, you will say that you gave a colleague [fem.] a ride from the airport. It will be true, the way a list of notes is true about a song.

### ch_09.008
It is a Tuesday. It is the last week of October. The window is down, the night is warm, and you are driving.

#### notes ch_09
- "la misma hilera paciente ... quién iba en ellas": plural "ellas" after singular "hilera" (the real antecedent, "luces", is not in the sentence).
- "diciéndose que está bien. Va a estar bien.": subject splits between "he (the passenger)" and "it (the situation)".
- "ella está dormida, o cerca": "o cerca" is elliptical; "o casi" reads more natural.
- "Lo dejas ahí.": the antecedent of "lo" is loose (the screen's offer? the HOME route?).
