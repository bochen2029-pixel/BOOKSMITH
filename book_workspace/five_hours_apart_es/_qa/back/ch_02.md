### ch_02.001
## II. Stemmons

### ch_02.002
Let's say that the message arrives on the Stemmons.

### ch_02.003
But first you have to get out of the parking garage, and that part you do yourself. The car could probably already do it alone. It can back out of a spot in reverse and find the way to the street on its own, and there are people who upload videos of it slipping through parking garages narrower than this one. But there is a difference between what a thing can do and what you are going to let it do, and the spiral ramp that climbs up from four levels below the street, the ticket barrier arm [lit. "la pluma del boleto"] and the blind right turn to get onto Commerce are three things that you still like to do with your own hands. You drive it up the spiral, between the same concrete pillars with the same scrapes at bumper height, and the barrier arm lifts, and you are already up and out, in the last of the light.

### ch_02.004
Downtown at seven belongs to the valets and to the cleaning crews and to the people who walk dogs in front of darkened lobbies. On a terrace someone laughs at the end of a story whose beginning you are never going to hear. Above the old Magnolia building, the red Pegasus stands upright with its wings raised, as it has done, in one form or another, since 1934.

### ch_02.005
At the first red light you give the wheel to the car. When the light changes, the wheel moves under your hands, and you let it, and then you take your hands away. Your job now is to watch, which turns out to be something different from driving. You see more.

### ch_02.006
On the screen beside you, the car draws the world again just as it understands it: the lanes as clean lines, the other cars as gray, smooth shapes without faces and without drivers, a blue ribbon laid out ahead to show where it plans to go. Every fraction of a second it imagines the next seconds in a dozen different ways and chooses one. You know more or less how it does it. You have read the papers [lit. articles] and you have argued about the architectures at lunchtime. Knowing it has not made it less strange. It is the closest thing you know to watching a thought.

### ch_02.007
The Stemmons takes you north, out of downtown. The warehouses of the Design District slide by on your left and, behind them, beyond the levee, the long darkness of the floodway [lit. relief channel]. Ahead, the brake lights come on in waves, a mile of red that shudders all at once, and then they let go. In the mirror, the sphere of the Reunion tower gets smaller and keeps shining.

### ch_02.008
This is the part of the day that belongs to no one. You are not at work and you are not yet at home, and nothing more is asked of you than to ride seated [lit. go seated] and let yourself be carried. You have always liked it more than you could explain to anyone.

### ch_02.009
You had plans for tonight, if the ones one makes with oneself count. On the desk at your house there is a yoke clamped to the edge and a throttle quadrant beside it, and tonight was going to be Heathrow. You fly simulators since you were twelve [= have flown simulators since age twelve], when the airport was the biggest thing in your world and the game was the only way to get into it. Nowadays you fly the big jets, and you fly them the way real pilots almost no longer do, by hand, and what you fly is the arrival. Tonight was going to be the long descent over the English Channel in the dark and the lights of London rising under the nose of the airplane, the river, the towers on the left, two-seven left with a crosswind if the weather wanted to put it there. The approach is the only part that has always interested you. Seven hours of ocean fly themselves.

### ch_02.010
Then the screen sounds [= chimes]. 7:06. Dana, who runs your group.

### ch_02.011
> *Are you still downtown? Iris Wren's flight was held [lit. stayed] two hours on the ground at Heathrow. Now it lands at 8:10, Terminal D. Her driver gave up and I have a kid with a fever. Any chance you pick her up? She is staying at the hotel on Elm, next to the office.*
>
> *And feed her. It's on us [lit. it runs on our account].*

### ch_02.012
Iris Wren, from the London office, who tomorrow at nine presents before the review committee, and whom you have never seen. To you she is what most people at the company are: a name at the top of the documents. Hers are among the good ones, careful and a little dry, written by someone who checks their own sums [lit. accounts]. Her profile photo is the size of a postage stamp. You could not pick her out in a crowd.

### ch_02.013
For about three seconds you resist. Heathrow was right there. Then the three seconds pass.

### ch_02.014
—Hey, Grok —you say. The assistant answers with the unhurried voice that you chose for it because it was the least anxious of the options—. Take me to DFW. Terminal D, arrivals.

### ch_02.015
The blue ribbon on the screen lifts off the lanes toward home, thinks it over, and lays itself down again curving west, toward the Carpenter freeway. The car, which has no opinion about anything, takes the new ribbon without comment. You dictate two words to Dana, *I go* [= I'll go / I'm going], and you watch how the lanes toward home continue north without you.

### ch_02.016
Crossing Irving, the towers of Las Colinas rise lit up and almost empty, with their logos shining for no one. Then the sky ahead changes. Over the airport the darkness turns the matte amber of a place that never switches off completely, and there they are, the landing lights that you made out from floor forty-one, close now. Four, five, strung toward the runways, so evenly spaced that they look like a single slow thought, thought with great care. Each one carries a hundred people, or two hundred, or three hundred. Each one of those people comes from somewhere, and someone receives them [lit. "la" = her, agreeing with "persona"; = meets them], or doesn't.

#### notes ch_02
- "la pluma del boleto": Mexican term for the barrier arm; may be opaque to other es-419 readers.
- "Vuelas simuladores desde los doce años": "volar simuladores" is a little odd; "vuelas en simuladores" / "piloteas simuladores" reads more natural.
- "la veintisiete izquierda con viento cruzado si el clima quería ponerlo": "si el clima quería ponerlo" reads slightly calqued.
- "la oscuridad se vuelve del ámbar mate de un lugar...": "se vuelve del ámbar" is a bit awkward ("se vuelve ámbar mate" / "se tiñe del ámbar").
