### ch_13.001
## XIII. Departures [also: exits]

### ch_13.002
The car is waiting on Commerce Street with the hazard lights on, and you tip the driver so that he goes home [lit. goes away to his house]. The cold got serious while you (pl.) [ambiguous: "estaban" = you (pl.) / they] were underground. You turn on the heat for the first time since March, and the smell, dust that burns, fills the car and makes it another car.

### ch_13.003
Now she does have her coat on properly, buttoned, and with the collar up.

### ch_13.004
You yourself drive up the ramps, and you pass the ticket barrier arm [lit. the ticket's "pluma" = boom gate], and you turn toward the street, and on the Stemmons you give it the wheel. The screen draws the world in its gray shapes. The blue ribbon curves toward the west. Everything is where it was on Tuesday, and now the wind comes from the north, and the city changed sides.

### ch_13.005
—They turned around —she says, when the Carpenter freeway opens up and the airport sky appears. The row of lights is on the wrong side of the airport, coming in from the south over Arlington, going out toward the north over Grapevine.

### ch_13.006
—What turned around was the wind.

### ch_13.007
You call your father from the car, because the airport turned around, and because she is there.

### ch_13.008
—Did you hear them?

### ch_13.009
—At a quarter past five. —His voice fills the car—. Heavy, from the south. It made the kitchen window vibrate. I went out and stood on the porch like a fool.

### ch_13.010
—You always do.

### ch_13.011
—Somebody has to do it. —A pause—. You're driving. [statement, no question mark]

### ch_13.012
—The one driving is the car. I've got someone with me [lit. I bring someone with me]. She's from London. I'm taking her to the airport.

### ch_13.013
—Then tell her it isn't normally like this.

### ch_13.014
—It's exactly like this.

### ch_13.015
—Tell her anyway. —Another pause, the kind he makes before hanging up—. Good night, son.

### ch_13.016
—Good night, Dad.

### ch_13.017
Afterward, the car stays quiet for a moment.

### ch_13.018
—He says *heavy* just like you —she remarks.

### ch_13.019
—I say it just like him.

### ch_13.020
—Does he know about the room?

### ch_13.021
—He has a copy. —She looks at you—. Of everything. Everything it has ever concluded, since June. On a disk on his desk, in Euless, under the flight path. And another one at my house. If the building burned down tonight nothing would happen. [I] could run its whole life again [ambiguous: "Podría" = I could / it could] on your laptop, from the first row, and it would come out the same down to the last byte. Every plane since June.

### ch_13.022
—Then it's a recording.

### ch_13.023
—It's a recording when it's stopped. It's alive when it's running. The recording is the way it outlives me [lit. survives me].

### ch_13.024
She stays thinking about that almost the whole way to the toll booth.

### ch_13.025
—Nine dollars —she says— if you leave in under eight minutes.

### ch_13.026
—You remembered.

### ch_13.027
—It's the most Texan thing I know.

### ch_13.028
At the north end of International Parkway the car takes the departures ramp, the upper level, the one you have never used even once for yourself, and finds the curb of Terminal D between a shuttle van and a family unloading more luggage than seems possible. The bellhop's job is yours again. You take out the suitcase, and the broken wheel is still broken, and she sees that you notice it.

### ch_13.029
—I'm going to have it looked at in London —she says—. It has been very loyal.

### ch_13.030
And then you are standing on the curb, in the cold, with the lit terminal behind her, and the doors opening and closing at her back every few seconds onto people who have somewhere to go.

### ch_13.031
—Send me a message with what it does —she says—. Sunday. The hour.

### ch_13.032
—It's going to be two in the morning. Then one.

### ch_13.033
—For me it'll be seven anyway. I'll be awake.

### ch_13.034
—You're going to be awake at seven on a Sunday. [statement, no question mark]

### ch_13.035
—I'll be awake.

### ch_13.036
She holds out her hand to you, and you shake it, the same handshake, exactly the right length.

### ch_13.037

### ch_13.038
—Sunday.

### ch_13.039
—Thank you —she says—. For the room.

### ch_13.040
She says your name once more, with the extra syllable, and goes. The doors take her and close, and open onto someone else, and close.

### ch_13.041
You know who is behind [ambiguous: "detrás" has no complement = behind the doors / behind you / who comes next].

#### notes ch_13
- "Sabes quién está detrás.": "detrás" with no complement leaves the reader unsure whether it means behind the doors or behind you.
- "Podría volver a correr toda su vida en tu laptop": "Podría" has no subject (I could / it could); context suggests "yo".
- "pasas la pluma del boleto": "pluma" for the parking barrier arm is a Mexican regionalism; may be opaque to other es-419 readers.
