### ch_07.001
## VII. Elm Street

### ch_07.002
Downtown at a quarter past eleven on a Tuesday is a stage after the play. The streets are warm and almost empty, and the traffic lights make their changes for no one. A man hoses down a terrace, and the smell of wet concrete crosses the street to meet you. A security guard in a lobby raises two fingers from his desk when you [sing.] pass, and you raise two back to him.

### ch_07.003
She walks with the coat over her arm, still, as if she had carried it so long that she already forgot that it is there. She walks the way she came out through the doors: reading everything.

### ch_07.004
—Is it always this empty?

### ch_07.005
—At night. In the daytime it's hotter.

### ch_07.006
—That isn't an answer.

### ch_07.007
—It's the only one that anyone from here gives.

### ch_07.008
A train comes along Pacific when you [sing.] cross it, two cars, lit up the way a kitchen is lit at three in the morning, with no one on board, and it glides toward the West End.

### ch_07.009
—Would you ever leave? —she asks—. Dallas [lit. from Dallas].

### ch_07.010
—I think about it the way I think about learning to play the piano.

### ch_07.011
—Meaning? [lit. "¿O sea?" = or be it?]

### ch_07.012
—Fondly [lit. with affection]. Every so many years.

### ch_07.013
She laughs, and the laugh goes up the empty street and doesn't come back.

### ch_07.014
And then you are underneath it [lit. him]. From floor forty-eight it was a toy, something you could have picked up. From the street it is forty feet of red neon on a steel drilling tower, turning so slowly that you have to watch for a while to be sure [masc.], two horses back to back, so that wherever you look there is always one that is coming toward you. The red sticks to everything: the sidewalk, the windows across the street, her face when she tips it back to look.

### ch_07.015
—From down here it's bigger.

### ch_07.016
—Almost everything is.

### ch_07.017
—It's turning.

### ch_07.018
—It has always turned.

### ch_07.019
She watches it make almost a complete turn. “I would have used it too,” she says. “If I came in flying. I would have aimed toward that.”

### ch_07.020
Back at the hotel, the doors are the revolving kind, and the lobby behind them is all marble and silence. She turns and holds out her hand to you, and you shake it, a handshake of the exact duration.

### ch_07.021
—At seven —she says.

### ch_07.022
—At seven.

### ch_07.023
—Thank you —she says—. For the sign.

### ch_07.024
The door turns, and takes her away, and turns again empty. All night you saw doors open and close on people you didn't know. This time you know who is behind it.
