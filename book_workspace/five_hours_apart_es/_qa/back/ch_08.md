### ch_08.001
## VIII. Four in the Morning

### ch_08.002
Upstairs, in a room on floor twenty-two with the curtains open, Iris Wren does not sleep.

### ch_08.003
She has taken off her shoes and nothing else. The suitcase is standing upright against the wall where the bellhop left it, tilted on its wheel. The coat is on the chair. The bed is still made, and she is sitting at the foot [lit. at the feet], with her hands in her lap, looking at the city.

### ch_08.004
From here the horse is almost at eye level, a few blocks away, turning. A train passes along Pacific, two cars, lit up and empty, and the buildings take it away. The highway runs white in one direction and red in the other. Far off, where he pointed, the sky over the airport is a matte amber that does not change.

### ch_08.005
Her cell phone is face down on the nightstand. She doesn't turn it over.

### ch_08.006
The horse turns round and round [lit. gives turns]. She watches it make one turn, and another, and if she is counting them the number doesn't matter. Once she looks at the coat on the chair, and once at the suitcase, and once at her own hands, and then she goes back to the city. She doesn't get up. She doesn't try to sleep. She knows how this goes.

### ch_08.007
At 4:31 by her body and 11:31 by the clock on the nightstand she is still there, with the horse turning and the city stretched out to its edges, and she is not waiting for anything, and nothing is about to arrive.

#### notes ch_08
- "donde él señaló": the protagonist, "tú" everywhere else, is here "él" (third person, from Iris's side); the shift is clear, but no pointing gesture appears in the earlier Spanish chapters.
- "y si las está contando el número no importa": a comma after "contando" would help the reading.
