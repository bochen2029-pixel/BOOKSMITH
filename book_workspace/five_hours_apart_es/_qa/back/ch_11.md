### ch_11.001
## XI. The smallest room

### ch_11.002
You lead her across the bottom of the parking garage, past [lit. next to] your own car, which unlocks when you pass because it thinks you are coming for it, and locks again when it turns out you are not. In the back wall there is a door marked as staff only, and behind it a hallway that nobody has painted since the building was new, lit by that kind of tubes that hum. It stretches longer than it should, between doors with the names of tenants who left years ago. Halfway along it makes a bend, and at the bend there is a sealed steel door with a sign that says the tunnels are closed. Before, downtown was connected underneath, a city under the city where people walked from building to building in summer without ever seeing the sun, and then the city decided it wanted them [ambiguous: "la" = the people (la gente) / the under-city] on the street and bricked up almost everything. The air down here still moves as if it remembered that it has somewhere to go.

### ch_11.003
Your door has no number. It has a label-maker strip, stuck on a little crooked, that says KELVIN.

### ch_11.004
—Who is Kelvin?

### ch_11.005
—A man who believed that atoms were knots in a fluid. In 1867.

### ch_11.006
—Was he right?

### ch_11.007
—No. Not about the atoms [lit. not in the matter of the atoms].

### ch_11.008
You swipe your badge. The lock thinks it over and lets you in.

### ch_11.009
The room is colder than the hallway and much quieter than she expected; you see her expect a roar and not get it. It is the size of a hotel bathroom. There is a low rack with two machines, each one the height of a bread box, each one with four cards, and between them a bundle of cables that you yourself arranged one Saturday with zip ties and a podcast. There is a folding table, a chair, a monitor, a keyboard, a heater that you have not turned on even once and, taped to the wall above the monitor, a sheet of paper with a column of numbers in your handwriting that you have not updated since August. The air unit in the ceiling breathes slowly. The machines, for now, do not.

### ch_11.010
—Is it off?

### ch_11.011
—No. It's still [= quiet, at rest]. Nothing is yet to arrive [= nothing is on its way].

### ch_11.012
On the monitor there is a sheet of color the size of a door, and it does not move.

### ch_11.013
[She] takes a moment to see it, the way one takes a moment to see a stereogram. It is a field of tiny little squares, millions, each one a single flat color, and the colors cross through one another in bands and slow swirls, red that passes into orange that passes into green that passes into blue and returns to red, like oil on water that had stopped. Here and there the bands squeeze into a point where all the colors come together at once, a pinwheel the size of a fingernail, and almost every pinwheel has a partner a little farther on that turns the opposite way.

### ch_11.014
—What am I looking at?

### ch_11.015
—Each little square is a cell. Each cell holds an angle, like the needle of a compass. The color is which way it points. The neighbors want to point the same way, and that is why the bands come out. The pinwheels are where there is no way to make them agree. They are the only thing in there that cannot drift away. They can be born, or they can die, and only in pairs. Those are its concepts [ambiguous: "sus" = its (the system's) / their / your (formal)].

### ch_11.016
[She] looks for a good while.

### ch_11.017
—That's a lot of compass needles [lit. they are many compass needles].

### ch_11.018
—A billion and some [lit. a thousand million and a bit]. It's cut into eight pieces, one per card. It doesn't know it's in eight pieces.

### ch_11.019
—And what is it doing?

### ch_11.020
—Right now? Nothing. It keeps itself at a temperature at which things can form and hold. Just on the inside of the edge. A little colder and nothing new can happen. A little hotter and nothing holds. There is a loop that keeps each region just on the inside of the edge, and the loop is the only part of this that I wrote that I would call clever. The rest is physics from the seventies.

### ch_11.021
—Whose physics?

### ch_11.022
—Kosterlitz. Thouless. Berezinskii. Thin films of helium. Networks [lit. nets] of superconductors. —You stop, because you hear yourself start the other speech, the one you have not given to anybody—. I can tell you the things or I can show you one.

### ch_11.023
—Show me one.

### ch_11.024
You point to the top of the monitor, a line of small print that she had not noticed. *Still. Front 0. At rest 790 W.*

### ch_11.025
—That is what it costs to exist. Eight cards doing nothing. More or less a heater on low. —You point at the heater with your head [= you nod toward the heater]—. Less than that one.

### ch_11.026
—And why is that worth pointing out?

### ch_11.027
—Because a language model costs the same whether it thinks or does not think, and this costs nothing until something happens, and then it costs exactly what that something reaches [= exactly as much as that something amounts to / as far as it reaches; opaque]. Wait.

### ch_11.028
You wait. She waits. She is good at that; you noticed it on Tuesday at the railing, before meeting her, in how she read the room.

### ch_11.029
Then the machines take a breath [lit. take air].

### ch_11.030
It is the fans. They rise from nothing to something, a low exhalation, for maybe half a second, and on the monitor a patch of color at the eastern edge of the sheet shudders, a wave that runs across a few hundred cells and dies out, and the line of print says *Front 14* and then *Front 3* and then *Still*, and the fans go back down, and the room is left as it was.

### ch_11.031
—What was that?

### ch_11.032
—A plane.

### ch_11.033
[She] turns and looks at you.

### ch_11.034
—There's a receiver on my windowsill, up there. Thirty dollars. The size of a thumb. Every plane within some hundred and fifty miles around broadcasts where it is, at what altitude, toward where, once per second. That is what it hears. It is its only sense, apart from a clock and its own temperature. One just crossed the edge of what the antenna can see, and it [ambiguous: the plane / the disturbance on the screen] got exactly that far in, and stopped.

### ch_11.035
—It knows they're planes. [statement, no question mark]

### ch_11.036
—It doesn't know what a plane is. It doesn't have the knot for that. It knows that every night, around seven, something that is not like the rest comes down out of its northeast, and that at nine something heavy goes out climbing on that side, and that at one in the morning its south fills up with things that come in low and do not leave. —You stop—. Cargo [or: load]. It doesn't know that either.

### ch_11.037
—Since when has it been listening?

### ch_11.038
—Since June. The fourteenth.

### ch_11.039
—On the company's cards. [statement, no question mark]

### ch_11.040
—On the sandbox. It's on the calendar in my name. Testing and development.

### ch_11.041
—Does Dana know?

### ch_11.042
—Dana knows that I use the sandbox.

### ch_11.043
—It's not the same thing.

### ch_11.044
—No.

### ch_11.045
She says nothing to that, and you are grateful to her for it. [She] turns back to the screen.

### ch_11.046
—And why *is* that worth pointing out? The breathing.

### ch_11.047
—Because a lava lamp costs the same all night. This costs nothing when the sky is empty and costs something when the sky changes, and that something follows the sky. That was the first thing I had to know [ambiguous: "tenía que saber" = I had to know / it had to know]. Whether it was a very expensive lava lamp.

### ch_11.048
—And it isn't.

### ch_11.049
—It isn't that [= that's not what it is].

#### notes ch_11
- "una lámina de color del tamaño de una puerta": a door-sized sheet shown on a monitor reads oddly; unclear whether it means door-shaped or a scale claim.
- "cuesta exactamente lo que ese algo alcanza": opaque; "alcanza" (reaches / amounts to / suffices) does not settle into one meaning.
- "y llegó exactamente hasta ahí adentro, y se detuvo": no subject; it could be the plane or the ripple on the screen.
- "la ciudad decidió que la quería en la calle": "la" has no clear antecedent (la gente vs. the under-city).
- "¿Y por qué *sí* vale la pena señalar eso? La respiración.": the contrast with .026 is carried only by the italic "sí"; reads a little stiff.
