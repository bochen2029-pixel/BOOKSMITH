### ch_14.001
## XIV. Forty-one, again

### ch_14.002
You don't drive home.

### ch_14.003
You take the Carpenter freeway back with the heat on and the window up and both hands on the wheel, and the skyline of the city appears ahead as always, and you look at it anyway. At the office the parking barrier arm [lit. "pluma"] lifts with your badge. The lobby guard is a different one, and he raises two fingers, and you raise two to him.

### ch_14.004
The elevator takes you up from the bottom of the parking garage, forty-five floors, and your ears do their thing. The floor is dark. When you go in, the sensors wake the lights ahead of you one block at a time, so you advance through the office inside a small island of light that moves with you, the opposite of Tuesday, when they were putting it to sleep behind you [ambiguous: "la" = the light / the office; subject unstated]. Your desk is where you left it. On the windowsill the receiver blinks once per second.

### ch_14.005
You sit down, and you open the laptop, and you bring the sheet up on the screen.

### ch_14.006
It is still. Front zero. Two knots on the east edge turning one against the other. Above the log [ambiguous: "Arriba del registro" = at the top of the log / up in the log], below the row that says *still* at 19:15, there is one more.

### ch_14.008
You look out the window. The city is crisp and cold and goes on to its edges. The freeways are white toward you and red going away. Very far off, beyond Irving, the airport glows with its matte amber, and now the lights come in from the south, in their patient row, each one of them someone who is waited for [lit. someone whom they wait for], or not.

### ch_14.009
At 21:52 a light rises from the amber, slowly, as the heavies do, and climbs toward the north, and begins its long turn toward the east, toward the Atlantic, toward a city it is going to reach while here it is still night. You follow it with your eyes all the way, until it is a star among the stars and then no longer. Forty-five floors below you, in a room nobody wanted, the fans breathed once and came back down. On the laptop a row has closed.

### ch_14.011
It knows that something heavy went out climbing toward the northeast, inside its window. That's all it knows. You too [ambiguous: = that is all you know too / you also know it].

### ch_14.012
It is a Friday. It is the last night of the out-of-phase week. On Sunday at two it is going to be one again [lit. they are going to go back to being one], and you are going to be in the room for that, and it [ambiguous: "él" = it, the field / he] too. Until then nothing is yet to arrive [= nothing is on its way]. Up here the lights turn themselves off when you stop moving, and you are not going to stop moving, for a good while.

#### notes ch_14
- "Arriba del registro, debajo de la fila que dice *quieto* a las 19:15, hay una más.": "arriba" and "debajo" in one sentence read as contradictory placement.
- "lo contrario del martes, cuando la iban poniendo a dormir detrás de ti": "la" lacks a clear antecedent (la luz? la oficina?) and the verb has no subject.
- "y él también": "él" for the field can momentarily read as a person (the father was the last "él" in ch_13).
- "Tú también.": elliptical; unclear whether it means "you know that too" or "that is all you know too".
