## V. Carpenter Freeway [lit. Carpenter Highway]

### ch_05.002
The car unlocks when you approach, and she watches the mirrors unfold with the polite interest of someone who has been awake for twenty hours. You settle the suitcase in the back, broken wheel and all. You yourself drive the car down the ramps of the parking garage, and you come out onto International Parkway heading north, and on the first long straight you give it the wheel [ambiguous: "le das el volante" = give it (the car) / give her the wheel], and she watches your hands let go of it.

### ch_05.024

She simply ran out of words in the language of polite conversation, and you let the car fill the silence with the sound of its tires. Las Colinas goes past again, the same lit towers that you passed two hours ago in the other direction, still shining for no one.

### ch_05.025

### ch_05.036

A bellhop takes away the suitcase with its broken wheel, and you feel, absurdly, a pang on handing it over, as if they had promoted you from stranger to porter and now they were firing you [ambiguous: "despidiendo" = firing you, or seeing you off].

#### notes ch_05
- Blocks 006 and 007 set speech in curly quotes “…” (speech that follows narration inside a paragraph), while speech-led paragraphs use the raya; a mixed system (ch_06 follows the same pattern), which some readers will notice.
- "en la primera recta larga le das el volante": "le" can be read as giving the wheel to her, not to the car.
- "como si ... ahora te estuvieran despidiendo": "despedir" splits between being fired and being seen off.
- "Consideras darle la respuesta fácil." then a quoted answer: unclear whether the quote is the easy answer or the one given instead.
- "de desconocido a maletero": "maletero" is a porter in Mexico but a car trunk in Spain and parts of South America.
