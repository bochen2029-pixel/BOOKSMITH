## Overall judgment

This reads, for the most part, as a book written in Spanish. The es-419 vocabulary is consistent and never
self-conscious; the tú narration in the present holds without stacked subject pronouns; Iris sounds quick and wry
and the narrator plain; the rows read as a machine's Spanish, not a gloss. Where the translation shows, it shows
at sentence level, not word level: a few dozen English-shaped turns, tense and relative-clause slips, and two
normative errors, all fixable with the minimal patches given. The real risk is architectural: several of the
author's word-for-word returns and charter-locked lines drifted at their later sites, which a reader would not
name but would feel as a less composed book. With the 2 BLOCK and 35 FIX lines applied, a Latin American reader
would take it for a Spanish original; the 24 NITs are polish.
