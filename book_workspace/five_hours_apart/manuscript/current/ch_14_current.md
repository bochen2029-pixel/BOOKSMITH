## XIV. Forty-One, Again

You don't drive home.

You take the Carpenter Freeway back in with the heat on and the window up and both hands on the wheel, and the skyline comes up ahead the way it always does, and you look at it anyway. At the office the garage arm lifts for your badge. The lobby guard is a different one, and lifts two fingers, and you lift two.

The elevator takes you up from the bottom of the garage, forty-five floors, and your ears do the thing. The floor is dark. As you walk in, the sensors wake the lights ahead of you one bank at a time, so that you move through the office inside a small moving island of light, the opposite of Tuesday, when they put it to sleep behind you. Your desk is where you left it. On the windowsill the receiver blinks once a second.

You sit, and open the laptop, and bring up the sheet.

It's quiet. Frontier zero. Two knots on the east edge turning against each other. At the top of the record, under the row that says *quiet* at 19:15, there's one more.

```
10-30 21:00:00  expect    N→NE heavy climbing  window 21:15–21:45
```

You look out the window. The city is sharp and cold and goes on to its edges. The freeways are white toward you and red away. Far out past Irving the airport glows its dull amber, and the lights come in from the south now, in their patient line, every one of them someone being met or not.

At 21:52 a light lifts out of the amber, slowly, the way the heavy ones do, and climbs north, and begins its long turn to the east, toward the Atlantic, toward a city it will reach while it's still dark here. You watch it the whole way, until it's a star among the stars and then not. Forty-five floors below you, in a room no one wanted, the fans have breathed once and gone down. On the laptop a row has closed.

```
10-30 21:52:31  expect    met  +22 min
10-30 21:52:32  quiet
```

It knows something heavy climbed out to the northeast, within its window. That's all it knows. So do you.

It's a Friday. It's the last night of the odd week. On Sunday at two it will be one again, and you'll be in the room for that, and so will it. Until then nothing is due. Up here the lights will go out on their own when you stop moving, and you don't, for a while.
