## V. Carpenter Freeway

The car unlocks as you approach, and she watches the mirrors unfold with the polite interest of someone who's been awake for twenty hours. You fit the suitcase in the back, broken wheel and all. You take the car down the garage ramps yourself, and out onto International Parkway heading north, and at the first long straight you give it the wheel, and she watches your hands leave it.

"Is it—"

"Driving itself. I'm supervising."

"Do you trust it?"

You consider giving her the easy answer. "About as much as I trust anything I mostly understand."

She thinks about that. "That's either very reassuring or not at all."

"Yes."

She laughs, once, a short surprised sound, and turns to watch the road with what you'd have to call professional interest.

At the north plaza the car slows, and the toll posts itself to the screen, and she reads it.

"Two dollars. To leave an airport."

"It's nine if you leave in under eight minutes. It's for people cutting through. It's the only place in Texas where it's cheaper to loiter."

"That's the most Texan thing I've heard."

"What do you work on?" she asks, a mile later. "Dana said evaluation, which could mean almost anything."

"Evaluation. I build the tests that tell us when a model is wrong."

"And confident about it."

"Those are the interesting ones."

"Find anything good lately?"

"Tonight, actually. Two processes that each thought they'd gotten there first."

"That's every meeting I've ever been in," she says, and you laugh harder than the line deserves, because it's the first joke between you and it landed.

"You're from here?"

"Euless. Under the airport. We were on the flight path. You get so you can't sleep without it."

"That explains the sign," she says, and doesn't explain what she means, and you don't ask.

After that she goes quiet, the long-flight kind of quiet. It isn't unfriendly. She's simply out of words in the language of small talk, and you let the car fill the silence with the sound of its tires. Las Colinas goes by again, the same lit towers you passed two hours ago going the other way, still glowing for no one.

Then the freeway bends, and Dallas comes up ahead all at once: the whole skyline standing up out of the dark, towers outlined in light, Reunion's globe glowing at the near end like a lantern someone left out on purpose.

"Oh," she says. Just that.

You've driven toward this view perhaps a thousand times. You look at it now because she's looking at it, and for a second you see it as she must: as a place nobody has explained to you yet.

"Dana said to feed you," you say.

"Did she."

"On the company."

"Well," Iris says. "I'd hate to disobey Dana."

"There's a restaurant at the top of your hotel."

"Is it terribly fancy?"

"It's terribly fancy."

"Good. I've had nothing but pretzels since somewhere over Ireland."

The car brings you down off the freeway into downtown, where the streets are emptier now, and stops itself at the hotel's curb. A bellman takes the suitcase with its broken wheel, and you feel, absurdly, a pang at handing it over, as if you'd been promoted from stranger to porter and are now being let go.
