## Three most serious problems
1. **ch_09.006 "und niemand wartet, und sagt sich" (BLOCK, wrong meaning).** "sagt sich" takes "niemand" as its
   subject: nobody tells themselves it's fine. In the English it is the person arriving alone. Fix: "ohne dass auf
   ihn gewartet wird, und sagt sich", which keeps "warten" and echoes "Auf jemanden … wird gewartet" just before.
2. **ch_09.006 "der Arm wird müde, wechselt den Arm" (BLOCK, grammar).** After the inserted clause "wechselt" has no
   subject of its own and reads "the arm switches the arm". Fix: "…, und er wechselt den Arm", which also restores
   III's tired-arm phrase (charter §12: one phrase).
3. **A wrong antecedent changes the first reading,** where German gender and case offer one the English never had:
   ch_03.007 (the forty minutes read as already over), ch_02.012 ("Rechnungen" read as invoices), ch_06.060 (the
   relative clause attaches to the speech), ch_09.007 (a colleague who works at the airport).

## Judgment
For most of its length Half A reads as a book written in German. The second-person present is idiomatic and the locked
lines sit naturally. The dialogue is the strongest part: Iris's dryness survives („Hat sie das.“, „Dann danke, dass du
freiwillig gemeldet worden bist.“), the father's terseness carries, and the engineer's jargon (Fix, Paper, ausgerollt,
live gegangen) sounds like a German engineer. The translation shows mainly in the long descriptive paragraphs of I to
III, where English word order and the English present perfect come through, and where loose English pronoun reference
was carried over and German gender and case turn it into wrong antecedents. The only real errors are the two BLOCKs,
both in one paragraph of IX. With those applied and most of the FIXes taken, Half A would pass as original German.
