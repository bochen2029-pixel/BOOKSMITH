## The three most serious problems
1. **ch_12.054 „Es war sehr warm.“ (BLOCK).** After „Im August.“ a reader takes it as the weather; the scene turns on
   the voice model being warm. Fix: „Es war sehr herzlich.“
2. **ch_14.011 „Mehr weiß es nicht. Du auch.“ (BLOCK).** After a negative German needs „auch nicht“; the chapter's
   key line was wrong. Fix: „Du auch nicht.“ The echo of XII .023 („Mehr wusste es nicht.“) survives.
3. **ch_12.117 „vor der Wende“ (FIX).** In German this means "before 1989". Fix: „Leg die Vorhersage fest, bevor der
   Flughafen umdreht.“ A close second: ch_10.021 „Bis hierhin und nicht weiter.“, a set phrase for drawing a line,
   spoken just as he opens up to her. Fix: „Weiter runter geht es nicht.“

## Decisions for the moderator (not filed as findings)
- „umdrehen“ is the book's verb for the airport switching direction; with planes as the subject it can read "turn
  back" (XIII .005, XII .097). Only the two worst places were filed (XII .094, XIII .006).
- „Etikettstreifen“ (XI .003 and the row glossary): the usual form is „Etikettenstreifen“; the two places echo each
  other, so change both or neither (the XI finding was withdrawn rather than break the echo).
- Dates: „Am neunten“ (XII .005) and „Dem vierzehnten“ (XI .038) are fine lowercase, the month just named; only XII
  .107, with no month nearby, takes „seit dem Neunten“.
- Rows: the only German error in the XV–XVIII rows is ch_17.006 („über der Mitte“ is above the midpoint, not past it).

## Judgment
For most of its length Half B reads as a book written in German. The narration is plain and exact and keeps the
author's long „und …, und …“ rhythm; the dialogue is quick, and the locked lines sit naturally. Part Three is a
convincing German machine log, Iris's XVIII monologue has her dry precision, and the father is terse and true except
for the bookish „Narr“. What gave the translation away was local and fixable: the idiom collisions (two changed the
meaning), the „es tun“ habit, and some calqued phrasing in the XI and XII explanations, where he should sound like a
German engineer talking. With the 2 BLOCKs and 24 FIXes applied, this half can go in front of a German reader without
apology.
