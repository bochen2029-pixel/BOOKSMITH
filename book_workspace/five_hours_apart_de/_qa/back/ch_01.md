# Back-translation (de-DE → EN), unit: ch_01 — Half A, blind

### ch_01.001
## I. Forty-One

### ch_01.002
Let's say it is a Tuesday in the last week of October, the slanted week [schräg = slanted/skewed; colloquially also "odd, off-kilter"], in which London is five hours ahead of Dallas instead of six, even if you have no reason yet to know that.

### ch_01.003
You are on the forty-first floor. Everyone else left an hour ago [perfect: "have gone"], and the motion detectors have put the office to sleep behind you, one row of lights after the other, until the only light on the floor is yours. Somewhere farther back in the corridor a vacuum cleaner runs, stops, runs again. The dark monitors on the empty desks around you each hold a pale likeness [Abbild = image/likeness, not literally "reflection"] of you ready, if you look, so you don't look.

### ch_01.004
What the firm does, in case someone asks you at a party, is move money. What it actually does is to move the information that moves the money, a few hundred million times a day, and your job is to make sure that the models that watch all this information tell the truth. You build the tests that catch them when they don't. It is a strange kind of work, if one wants to explain it. Mostly you don't even try.

[grammatically ambiguous: "die Tests, die sie erwischen" = "the tests that catch them" or "the tests that they catch"; context favors the tests catching the models]

### ch_01.005
What you are looking at is a test. For a week it passes [= has been passing] forty-nine of fifty times, and on the fiftieth time it fails, without anyone finding a reason, which means that there is a reason and only no one has found it yet. You have taken it along to lunch and to the gym and under the shower, where you do most of your actual thinking. This afternoon you have carried it through two meetings, in which you nodded at the right places and heard almost nothing.

### ch_01.006
At 6:52 you find it [ambiguous: "ihn" (masc.) = the reason (Grund) or the test]. Two processes, each convinced it had been there first. Almost always one of the two is right. On one in fifty runs they cross each other in the dark, and everything downstream tips over, in a way that looks like an error of the model and isn't one.

### ch_01.007
The fix is four lines long. You write them and let the test run fifty times, then two hundred times, and watch the results light up green, line after line of identical successes that fill the screen like windows that come on in a high-rise at dusk.

### ch_01.008
No one will ever learn which four lines. Sometime tomorrow someone will notice that the test no longer fails, and assume it repaired itself, the way one assumes that the light comes on by itself. That is all right. That is the part that you would do for free [umsonst = for free; also "in vain"]. The part that you would do if no one paid you and no one ever found out about it: the moment in which something that was wrong [falsch = wrong / false] simply becomes true under your hands.

### ch_01.009
You lean back, and the chair creaks, and for the first time in hours you look out of the window.

### ch_01.010
The city has lit up while you were not looking. The freeways run white toward you and red away from you, braided where they cross, never quite still. West of downtown the Trinity is a long, drawn-out nothing, the only dark thing in the picture. The ball of the Reunion Tower slowly goes through its light patterns. And farther out, behind everything, the landing lights hang in their patient rows in the dark.

### ch_01.011
Two rows, strictly speaking. You can tell them apart without straining. The low ones, near and dense, belong to Love Field: 737s that come down every few minutes over Bachman Lake, just as [they have] your whole life long. The high ones, far out beyond Irving, slow and evenly spaced, belong to DFW. Tonight south wind, so they land on runways seventeen and eighteen and come in over Grapevine. You know that the way you know your own address. You grew up in Euless, in the Mid-Cities, under the airport, and the sound of a heavy on final approach at three thousand feet is the nearest thing to a lullaby that you ever had.

### ch_01.012
For years you see [= have been seeing] these lights from this window. Not one single time have you asked yourself who sits in them.

### ch_01.013
On the windowsill a black receiver, no bigger than a thumb, blinks once per second, as [it has] since June, and listens into a sky that it cannot see. You do not look at it.

### ch_01.014
You pack up the laptop. You find your ID badge [Ausweis = ID card / pass / badge]. The elevator takes you soundlessly forty-one floors down, and halfway down your ears do that little thing that they do at the end of a flight.

### ch_01.015
P4 lies four levels below the street, all the way down in the underground garage, where there is always a space free and the air never moves. It smells of concrete and cooling engines. Your car sees you coming and unlocks, and its mirrors fold out like something that is waking up. Before you have touched anything, the screen has already guessed: HOME, in white letters on the map, because it is after six on a workday and it [es = the car (neuter Auto); the screen, Bildschirm, would be "er"] has learned what you do on workdays after six. Twenty-five minutes, it says, straight up the Stemmons [lit. "always up the Stemmons"], and mostly it is right.

#### notes ch_01
- "der schrägen Woche": "schräg" for the time-zone-shifted week is unusual; most readers will hear "weird/odd", fine, but the image is slightly forced.
- "halten jeder ein blasses Abbild von dir bereit": "Abbild" is a likeness/copy, not a reflection ("Spiegelbild"); reads a little stiff.
- "das Nächste an einem Schlaflied, das du je hattest": calque of "the closest thing to a lullaby"; natural German would be "das, was einem Schlaflied am nächsten kommt".
- "Seit Jahren siehst du diese Lichter ... wer in ihnen sitzt": "in ihnen sitzt" (sits in the lights) is a deliberate metonymy but reads odd in German.
- "dass es an einem Werktag nach sechs ist und es gelernt hat": two "es" in a row with different referents (impersonal, then the car); momentarily confusing after "der Bildschirm".
