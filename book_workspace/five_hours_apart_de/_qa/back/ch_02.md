### ch_02.001
## II. Stemmons

### ch_02.002
Let's say the message [Nachricht = message / news] comes on the Stemmons.

### ch_02.003
But first you have to get out of the underground garage, and that you do yourself. The car could probably do it alone by now. It can back out of a space and find the way to the street itself, and people post videos of how it threads its way through tighter parking garages than this one. But there is a difference between what a thing can do and what you let it do, and the spiral ramp up from four levels below the street, the ticket barrier and the blind [unübersichtlich = hard to see around] right-hand curve onto Commerce are three things that you still like to do with your own hands. You drive up the spiral, past the same concrete pillars with the same scrapes at bumper height, and the barrier lifts, and you are up and out in the last light.

### ch_02.004
Downtown at seven belongs to the parking valets and the cleaning crews and people who walk their dogs past dark lobbies. On a terrace someone laughs at the end of a story whose beginning you will never hear. Above the old Magnolia Building stands the red Pegasus with raised wings, as [it has] since 1934, in one form or another.

### ch_02.005
At the first red light you hand the steering over to the car. When the light changes, the steering wheel moves under your hands, and you let it [ambiguous: "es" = the steering wheel (Lenkrad) or the car (Auto)], and then you take your hands away. Your task now is the watching, and that is, as it turns out, something other than driving. You see more.

### ch_02.006
On the screen beside you the car draws the world anew, the way it understands it: lanes [Spuren = lanes / tracks / traces] as clean lines, other cars as smooth gray shapes without faces and without drivers, a blue stripe laid out in front of you to show where it wants to go. In every fraction of a second it imagines the next seconds in a dozen different ways and picks one. You know roughly how it does that. You have read the papers and argued about the architectures over lunch. The knowing has not made it one bit less strange. It is the thing that comes closest to watching a thought [lit. "to the watching-at a thought"].

### ch_02.007
The Stemmons carries you north out of the core. On the left the warehouses of the Design District glide past, and behind them, beyond the levee, the long dark of the floodplain [Überflutungsgebiet = flood zone / floodplain]. Ahead of you the brake lights come on in waves, a mile of red that flinches all at once and then lets go again. In the mirror the ball of the Reunion Tower grows smaller and keeps on shining.

### ch_02.008
This is the part of the day that belongs to no one. You are no longer at work and not yet at home, and nothing is asked of you except to sit here and let yourself be carried. You have always liked it more than you could explain to anyone.

### ch_02.009
You had plans for tonight, if the kind counts that one makes with oneself. On the desk at home a yoke [Steuerhorn] is clamped to the edge and next to it a throttle quadrant, and tonight Heathrow would have been up [= would have been next]. You fly [= have been flying] simulators since you were twelve, when the airport was the biggest thing in your world and the game the only way to get in. Today [heute = today / nowadays] you fly the big jets, and you fly them the way the real pilots mostly no longer do, by hand, and what you fly is the arrival. Tonight it would have been the long descent over the English Channel in the dark and the lights of London coming up under the nose, the river, the high-rises on the left, the Twenty-Seven Left with a crosswind, if the weather wanted one. The approach is the only part that has ever interested you. Seven hours of ocean fly themselves.

### ch_02.012

For you she is what most people in the firm are: a name at the top of documents. ### ch_02.013

### ch_02.014

### ch_02.015
The blue stripe on the screen lifts itself off the lanes toward home, considers, and lies down again, bent toward the west, toward the Carpenter Freeway. The car, which has an opinion about nothing, takes the new stripe without comment. You dictate two words back to Dana, *Will do* [lit. "Do I" = "I'll do it"], and watch the lanes toward home go on running north without you.

### ch_02.016
Through Irving the towers of Las Colinas stand lit up and mostly empty, their logos shine for no one. Then the sky ahead of you changes. Above the airport the dark turns into the dull amber-yellow of a place that never quite goes out, and there they are, the landing lights that you sorted from the forty-first floor, now close. Four, five of them, lined up toward the runways, so evenly spaced that they look like a single slow thought that is being thought very carefully. Each of them carries a hundred people, or two hundred, or three hundred. Each of these people comes from somewhere and is picked up, or not.

#### notes ch_02
- "Dann ertönt ein Ton vom Bildschirm": tautological ("a tone sounds"); "ertönt ein Signal" or "kommt ein Ton" would be smoother.
- "Durch Irving stehen die Türme von Las Colinas erleuchtet": "durch" + "stehen" is odd (towers cannot stand "through" Irving); the sense "as you pass through Irving" has to be inferred.
- "geschrieben von einer, die ihre eigenen Rechnungen nachprüft": "Rechnungen" reads first as bills/invoices for many readers; "Berechnungen" would be unambiguous.
- "Heute fliegst du die großen Jets": "heute" (today vs. nowadays) collides with the surrounding "heute Abend".
- "die Hochhäuser links, die Siebenundzwanzig links": the two "links" (on the left / runway Left) sit side by side and can blur.
