### ch_13.001
## XIII. Departure [Abflug = departure by air / takeoff]

### ch_13.002
The car is waiting on the Commerce with its hazard lights switched on, and you give the driver a tip so that he can drive home. While you (pl.) were downstairs, the cold has turned serious. You switch on the heating for the first time since March, and its smell [ambiguous: "ihr Geruch" = its (the heating's) smell, or her smell], burning dust, fills the car and makes it into a different car.

### ch_13.003
She has the coat properly on now, buttoned up, the collar turned up.

### ch_13.004
You drive up the ramps yourself, and through the ticket barrier, and around the curve onto the street, and on the Stemmons you hand the wheel over to it [ambiguous: "ihm" = it (the car) or him]. The screen draws the world in its gray shapes. The blue stripe turns off to the west. Everything is where it was on Tuesday, and the wind now comes from the north, and the city has changed sides.

### ch_13.005
"They have turned around," she says, as the Carpenter Freeway opens up and the sky over the airport comes into view. The row of lights is on the wrong side of the airport, comes in from the south over Arlington and climbs out to the north over Grapevine.

### ch_13.006
"The wind has turned around."

### ch_13.007
You call your father from the car, because the airport has turned around and because she is there.

### ch_13.008
"Did you hear them?" [ambiguous: "sie" = them (the planes) or her]

### ch_13.009
"Quarter past five." His voice fills the car. "A heavy, from the south. Made the kitchen window rattle. I went out and stood on the porch like a fool."

### ch_13.010
"You do that every time."

### ch_13.011
"Somebody has to do it, after all." A pause. "You're driving."

### ch_13.012
"The car is driving. I have someone with me. She is from London. I am taking her to the airport."

### ch_13.013
"Then tell her that it is not like this otherwise [= that it isn't usually like this]."

### ch_13.014
"It is always exactly like this."

### ch_13.015
"Tell her anyway." Again a pause, the kind he makes before he hangs up. "Good night, boy." [Junge = boy/lad/son]

### ch_13.016
"Good night, Dad."

### ch_13.017
Afterward it is still [= quiet] in the car for a moment.

### ch_13.018
"He says *heavy* exactly like you," she says.

### ch_13.019
"I say it like him."

### ch_13.020
"Does he know about the room?"

### ch_13.021
"He has a copy." She looks at you. "Of the whole thing. Everything that it has inferred since June. On a hard drive in his desk, in Euless, under the approach path. And one with me. If the building burned down tonight, it would not matter. I could run its [ambiguous: "sein" = its (the field's) or his] whole life again on your laptop, from the first line on, and it would come out exactly the same down to the byte. Every airplane since June."

### ch_13.022
"Then it is a recording."

### ch_13.023
"It is a recording when it is stopped. It lives when it runs. The recording is how it outlives me."

### ch_13.024
She thinks about it almost the whole way to the toll booth.

### ch_13.025
"Nine dollars," she says, "if one drives out in less than eight minutes."

### ch_13.026
"You remembered it."

### ch_13.027
"It is the most Texan thing that I know."

### ch_13.028
At the north end of the International Parkway the car takes the ramp to Departures, the upper roadway, which you have never once used for yourself, and finds a place at the curb at Terminal D, between a shuttle and a family that is unloading more luggage than seems possible. The bellhop's work is yours again. You lift the suitcase out, and the broken wheel is still broken, and she sees that you notice it.

### ch_13.029
"I will have it looked at in London," she says. "It has been very loyal." [masculine "ihn"/"Er" = the suitcase (Koffer); reads as "him"/"he"]

### ch_13.030
Then you stand in the cold at the curb, behind her the lit-up terminal, and behind her the doors slide open and shut every few seconds, for people who have to get somewhere.

### ch_13.031
"Write me what it does," she says. "Sunday. The hour."

### ch_13.032
"Then it is two o'clock in the morning. Then one."

### ch_13.033
"Where I am [lit. "With me"] it is seven either way. I am awake."

### ch_13.034
"You are awake at seven on a Sunday."

### ch_13.035
"I am awake."

### ch_13.036
She holds out her hand to you, and you take it, the same handshake, exactly the right length.

### ch_13.037
"Sunday," she says.

### ch_13.038
"Sunday."

### ch_13.039
"Thank you," she says. "For the room."

### ch_13.040
She says your name once more, with the extra syllable [lit. "with the syllable more"], and goes. The doors take her in and close, and open for someone else, and close.

### ch_13.041
You know who is behind them. [ambiguous: "dahinter" = behind the doors, or idiomatically "behind it" = responsible]

#### notes ch_13
- „Hast du sie gehört?“ (ch_13.008): "sie" can be read as "her" as easily as "them" (the planes).
- „Ich könnte sein ganzes Leben noch einmal ... laufen lassen“ (ch_13.021): right after talk of the father, "sein" reads first as "his" rather than the field's.
- „Er hat eine Kopie.“ Sie sieht dich an. „Von dem Ganzen. ...“ (ch_13.021): the beat "Sie sieht dich an" inside the speaker's own line makes the attribution of the speech briefly confusing.
- "und ihr Geruch, verbrennender Staub" (ch_13.002): "ihr" can be misread as "her smell".
- „Du weißt, wer dahinter ist.“ (ch_13.041): echoes the idiom "wer dahintersteckt" (who is responsible), which muddies the plain "behind the doors" sense.
