# Back-translation (de-DE → EN), unit: ch_03 — Half A, blind

### ch_03.001
## III. International Parkway

### ch_03.002
Let's say it is eight minutes to eight when you come through the southern toll plaza and drive along the International Parkway.

### ch_03.003
The airport is bigger than Manhattan. You know [= have known] that your whole life as a curiosity, and now you feel it, where [= now that] the road leads right through the middle of the grounds and the terminals curve away on both sides, each a long lit-up half-moon, like ships that are moored at a dark canal. They were built as semicircles so that the way from the car to the gate would be short, by people who assumed everyone would always come by car. You know this place the way one knows a house that one grew up in. You know that the train runs inside security and the bus outside, that the parking garages stand inside the half-moons, that D lies on the left when one drives north, that the cell phone lot fills up by eight on a Friday. Outside beyond the terminals airplanes move slowly and deliberately across the airfield [Rollfeld = tarmac / movement area], as if the ground were deep water.

### ch_03.004
You park the car on the fourth level of the Terminal D parking garage, between a minivan and a black sedan [Limousine = sedan in standard German; also "limousine"] whose driver is sleeping with the seat back fully reclined, and go inside.

### ch_03.005
Inside it is the hour in which an airport ends its performance [Vorstellung = performance / show]. The shops pull down their grilles. A man on a floor-polishing machine draws slow loops across the terrazzo. The announcements come less often and sound as though they were addressed to an empty room. Up on the board, next to LONDON HEATHROW, the word DELAYED flickers and becomes LANDED 8:14 and then, a while later, AT GATE.

### ch_03.006
She will have come [= has presumably come] on a Triple Seven, the big one, the one with the swept [gepfeilt = swept / raked] wingtips, and the choreography you know from here on. The walk out of the jet bridge. The lines at passport control, short at this hour, but never empty. The waiting at the baggage carousel. Then the frosted-glass doors. Forty minutes, if everything goes smoothly, and you have never yet experienced everything going smoothly.

### ch_03.007
So you have forty minutes and a long day behind you [ambiguous: "hinter dir" can govern both nouns; the sense is forty minutes ahead and a long day behind], and you find the vending machines down by baggage claim, lit blue in a niche like a small aquarium. You press the buttons for the orange can, Ultra Sunrise, and it falls down with that heavy, hollow sound and is so cold that it hurts a little in your hand. You open it right away, standing. The hiss. The first long swallow, citrus and cold and light, pleasantly chemical, like something that was built in a lab by people who liked you. Three minutes later it arrives behind your eyes, a lifting, a brightening, as if someone had turned up the gain [Verstärkung = amplification / gain] in the whole terminal. You weren't tired, and now you are even less tired than that. It is a small pleasure, a cheap one and a completely reliable one.

### ch_03.008
International arrivals come out downstairs, through a pair of frosted-glass doors that slide open every minute or two, release a few people blinking into the light, and close again. In front of the doors, behind a railing, a crowd has gathered. There are drivers in dark suits with tablets on which names are written. There is a family with a homemade sign of purple glitter, an old man in an ironed shirt with supermarket flowers, still in their plastic sleeve, and two little boys who take turns climbing onto the railing until their mother stops them and they start again.

### ch_03.009
Every time the doors open, the whole crowd leans forward. Every time some face changes.

### ch_03.010
A young woman comes out, and the old man with the flowers makes a sound that you should not hear [ambiguous: "solltest" = should not / were not meant to], and you look away, and then you look back. A teenager walks straight past his mother, gets ten steps, then he turns around and comes back into her arms, as if he had only pretended not to know her. The boys' father comes out in a crumpled suit and goes down on one knee, and they slam into him with full force, and he lets them knock him over.

### ch_03.011
Everyone here is waiting for someone whom they love.

### ch_03.012
You are waiting for a stranger [eine Fremde = a female stranger].

### ch_03.013
When you were little, your father drove [perfect: "has driven"] with you on Saturday mornings to the north side of the airport, to the viewing area, where the loudspeakers broadcast the tower, and you (pl.) both sat on the hood and watched how they came in two hundred yards away, while a calm voice above you (pl.) told each single one that it was cleared for landing. He knew the airplanes by their sounds. He said *Heavy* before you could see it, and he was always right. Years later, when you came home from the year in Singapore, with everything you owned in two suitcases, he was standing at this railing, in a jacket that he had put on specially, and he had already been standing there an hour, because he did not trust the traffic. At night he doesn't drive anymore. He says it is because of the eyes. You never asked him how long he had stood here [perfect: "has stood"], and he never said it, and now, where [= now that] you are standing where he stood, you understand that it would not have bothered him.

### ch_03.014
You take out your phone. You open a blank note, type IRIS WREN and make the letters as big as they will go. Then you hold it up at chest height and feel exactly as ridiculous as expected [lit. "as thought"]. One of the drivers in the dark suits throws a glance at you, at your phone, and gives you a short, professional nod, as one colleague to the other.

### ch_03.015
The doors open. Close. Open. A balloon slips out of someone's fist and rises to the ceiling, forty feet up, where it stays. Your arm gets tired, and you switch arms, and you notice that none of it bothers you. Every time the doors open and someone is found, a little bit of it reaches you secondhand, a borrowed share in every reunion in the hall. By now you have had dozens.

#### notes ch_03
- "Also hast du vierzig Minuten und einen langen Tag hinter dir": "hinter dir" can attach to both objects, so the forty minutes can momentarily read as already past.
- "ein alter Mann in einem gebügelten Hemd mit Supermarktblumen": attachment ambiguity (a shirt with a flower print?) until "noch in ihrer Plastikhülle" resolves it.
- "ein Geräusch, das du nicht hören solltest": "solltest" reads as "should not" or "were not meant to"; both fit.
- "Das weißt du dein Leben lang als Kuriosität": present tense with "dein Leben lang" is slightly awkward ("schon dein ganzes Leben" would be more natural).
- "einer schwarzen Limousine": German "Limousine" is a sedan; some readers will picture a limo instead.
