### ch_10.001
## X. North Wind

### ch_10.002
Let's say it is Friday, the thirtieth, the last workday of the skewed week [ambiguous: "schräg" = slanted/oblique, or colloquially weird/offbeat], and at twenty to five the first north wind of the year moves through.

### ch_10.003
From the forty-first floor you see it before you feel it. The sky in the northwest takes on the color of a nickel coin. The flags on the plaza beyond the Commerce [= Commerce Street], which have hung down limp the whole week, lift and stretch and point to the south, all at once, like something that has decided. On the screen the outside temperature falls in forty minutes to 79, 71, 64, 57 °F, and the windows tick as they cool. Out behind Irving you know, without looking, what the airport is doing. It turns around. Every airplane over the Metroplex is told, one after the other, in a calm voice, that it will now land from the south. The row of lights that you have watched the whole week will form anew on the other side, over Euless, over the house in which you grew up, where your father at a quarter past five will hear the first heavy [German has the English loanword "Heavy"] come over and will know what it means, even before the weather does.

### ch_10.004
The week has run the way weeks run when they run well [ambiguous: "ist gelaufen" = has gone/run, or the idiom "is over, a done deal"], that is, fast and without much that one could show. On Wednesday at seven o'clock in the morning you (pl.) both sat at your desk with bad coffee and found the sixes. There were fourteen. The last one was stuck in a configuration file that nobody had opened since May, and when she found it, she said nothing at all, only turned the screen to you and laid her finger on it. At nine she stood before the committee [Gremium = committee/board/panel], with a slide with the title *What We Have Learned* in the place where the hole had been, and they asked two questions, and she answered three. On Thursday there was a dinner with the whole group at a place in Deep Ellum where one could hear nothing, and you said nothing and watched from the other end of the table how good she is at it. Today were meetings [= today was meetings]. Her suitcase is already standing at the reception of the hotel on [lit. "in the"] Elm. Her flight is the last one to London this night, from Terminal D at twenty to ten, and for a quarter past seven a car is ordered, a real one this time.

### ch_10.005
She comes to your desk at half past six, her coat on.

### ch_10.006
"It has paid for itself [= earned its keep]," she says. [ambiguous: masculine "Er" = the coat (Mantel, just mentioned), or possibly the north wind / the trip]

### ch_10.007
"It has." [lit. "Has it."]

### ch_10.008
"Fifty-five degrees. I was promised Texas." [lit. "To me was promised Texas."]

### ch_10.009
"This is Texas. The other one."

### ch_10.010
She looks out of the window at the city, which in the cold has become sharp-edged and clear, every light sharper than before. The flags are still standing horizontal.

### ch_10.011
"I was told a car comes at a quarter past." [lit. "To me was told, at quarter past comes a car."]

### ch_10.012
"It does." [lit. "Comes it."]

### ch_10.013
Since Tuesday you have been weighing it back and forth [lit. "you consider back and forth"], and now you notice that you already decided a while ago, without telling yourself.

### ch_10.014
"Before you go," you say. "Can I show you something? It is downstairs. Twenty minutes."

### ch_10.015
"Is it a secret?"

### ch_10.016
"It is a sandbox."

### ch_10.017
"That is not a no."

### ch_10.018
"No," you say. "It isn't."

### ch_10.019
The elevator takes you (pl.) forty-one floors down, and your ears do what they do, and then it keeps going. Past L. P1, P2, P3. P4, where your car stands. The doors open onto the bottom of the underground garage, lit the way the floors of underground garages are lit, and the air does not move.

### ch_10.020
"How much farther does it go down?" she asks.

### ch_10.021
"This far and no farther. The company rents the smallest room on the lowest level. It was cheap because nobody wanted it."

#### notes ch_10
- "Fünfundfünfzig Grad" (ch_10.008) has no unit; a German reader defaults to Celsius, so the line reads oddly unless the earlier "°F" is carried in mind.
- "fällt die Außentemperatur in vierzig Minuten auf 79, 71, 64, 57 °F" (ch_10.003): "auf" with a list of four values reads awkwardly; "über ... auf" or a dash sequence would be clearer.
- "der schrägen Woche" (ch_10.002): "schräg" is ambiguous (slanted vs. colloquial "weird"); the intended sense is unclear.
- "das erste Heavy herüberkommen hören" (ch_10.003): English aviation jargon "Heavy" is opaque to a general German reader.
- „Er hat sich bezahlt gemacht“ (ch_10.006): the referent of "Er" is not obvious; the nearest masculine noun is "Mantel".
