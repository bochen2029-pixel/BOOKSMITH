# Back-translation (de-DE → EN), unit: ch_08 — Half A, blind

### ch_08.001
## VIII. Four in the Morning

### ch_08.002
Upstairs, in a room on the twenty-second floor with open curtains, Iris Wren is not sleeping.

### ch_08.003
She has taken off her shoes and nothing else. The suitcase stands against the wall where the bellhop set it down, crooked on its broken wheel. The coat lies on the chair. The bed is still made, and she sits on its foot end, her hands in her lap, and looks at the city.

### ch_08.004
From here the horse is almost at eye level, a few blocks away, and turns. A light-rail train runs along the Pacific, two cars, lit and empty, and the buildings take it in. The freeway runs white in one direction and red in the other. Far out, where he pointed [ambiguous: "er" = he (a male person, presumably the narrator) or it (der Freeway, masc.)], the sky above the airport is a dull amber-yellow that does not change.

### ch_08.005
Her phone lies display-down on the nightstand. She does not turn it over.

### ch_08.006
The horse turns. She watches it make one round, and another, and if she is counting along, the number does not matter. Once she looks at the coat on the chair, and once at the suitcase, and once at her own hands, and then at the city again. She does not get up. She does not try to sleep [ambiguous without comma: "Sie versucht nicht zu schlafen" = does not try to sleep / tries not to sleep]. She knows how it goes.

### ch_08.007
At 4:31 by her body and 11:31 by the clock on the nightstand she is still there, and the horse turns, and the city lies spread out to its edges, and she is waiting for nothing, and nothing is pending [lit. "nothing stands on"; anstehen = be due / be scheduled / be coming up].

#### notes ch_08
- "Weit draußen, wohin er gezeigt hat": "er" reads either as the freeway or as a male "he"; if it is the narrator, this genders the "du" as male, and no pointing gesture appears in the earlier chapters of this half.
- "Sie versucht nicht zu schlafen.": without a comma it can mean "tries not to sleep" as well as "does not try to sleep".
- "und nichts steht an": "anstehen" (be pending / on the agenda) is a slightly office-like register for a lyrical close.
