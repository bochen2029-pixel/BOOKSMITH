### ch_11.001
## XI. The Smallest Room

### ch_11.002
You lead her across the bottom of the underground garage, past your own car, which unlocks as you walk past because it thinks you are coming to get it, and locks again when you don't. On the back wall there is a door that is labeled as a staff door, and behind it a corridor that nobody has painted since the building was new, lit by the sort of tubes that hum. It reaches farther than it should, past doors with the names of tenants who moved out years ago. Halfway along it bends off, and at the bend there is a sealed steel door with a sign on which it says that the tunnels are closed. The downtown used to be connected underground, a city under the city, in which people in summer went from building to building without ever seeing the sun, and then the city decided that it wanted them on the street [ambiguous: second "sie" = the people, or possibly the tunnels' traffic], and walled up most of it. The air down here still moves, as if it remembered a place to which it could go.

### ch_11.003
Your door has no number. It has a label strip, stuck on slightly crooked, on which KELVIN stands [= is written].

### ch_11.004
"Who is Kelvin?"

### ch_11.005
"A man who thought atoms were knots in a fluid. 1867."

### ch_11.006
"Was he right?"

### ch_11.007
"No. Not about the atoms." [lit. "Not with the atoms."]

### ch_11.008
You pull your ID card through [= swipe your badge]. The lock considers briefly and lets you [= lets you in].

### ch_11.009
The room is colder than the corridor and much quieter than she thought; you can see on her [= in her face] that she reckoned with a roar and gets none. It is as big as a hotel bathroom. There is a low rack with two machines in it, each as high as a bread box, each with four cards, and between them a cable harness that you laid yourself one Saturday, with cable ties and a podcast. There is a folding table, a chair, a monitor, a keyboard, a fan heater that you have never switched on, and, taped to the wall above the monitor, a sheet of paper with a column of numbers in your handwriting, which [ambiguous: "die" = the column or the numbers] you have not updated since August. The ventilation in the ceiling breathes slowly. The machines are not doing it at the moment.

### ch_11.010
"Is it off?"

### ch_11.011
"No. It is still [still = quiet/silent/motionless]. Nothing is pending [lit. "Nothing stands on"]."

### ch_11.012
On the monitor there is a color surface [Farbfläche = area/surface of color], as big as a door, and it does not move.

### ch_11.013
She needs a moment to see it, the way one needs a moment to see a stereogram. It is a field of tiny squares, millions of them, each a single flat color, and the colors run into one another in slow bands and swirls, red into orange into green into blue and back to red, like oil on water that has stood still [= that has stopped moving; ambiguous: the oil or the water]. Here and there the bands draw together to a point at which all the colors meet at once, a pinwheel [Windrad = pinwheel or wind turbine] as big as a fingernail, and almost every pinwheel has a counterpart a small way off that turns the other way around.

### ch_11.014
"What am I seeing there?"

### ch_11.015
"Every square is a cell. Every cell holds an angle, like a compass needle. The color is the direction in which it points. Neighbors want to point in the same direction, that is why there are the bands. The pinwheels are the places where one cannot bring it into agreement. Those are the only things in it that cannot drift. They can be born or die, and only in pairs. Those are its concepts [ambiguous: "seine Begriffe" = its (the system's) or his terms/concepts/words]."

### ch_11.016
She looks at it for a long time.

### ch_11.017
"Those are a lot of compass needles."

### ch_11.018
"A billion and a bit. It is cut into eight pieces, one per card. It does not know that it consists of eight pieces."

### ch_11.019
"And what does it do?"

### ch_11.020
"Right now? Nothing. It is held at a temperature at which things can form and hold. Just inside the edge. A bit colder, and nothing new can happen. A bit warmer, and nothing holds. There is a loop that holds every region just inside the edge, and the loop is the only part of it that I wrote and that I would call clever. The rest is physics from the seventies."

### ch_11.021
"Whose physics?"

### ch_11.022
"Kosterlitz. Thouless. Berezinskii. Thin helium films. Lattices of superconductors." You stop, because you hear yourself starting the other speech, the one that you have given to no one. "I can tell you the things, or I can show you one."

### ch_11.023
"Show me one."

### ch_11.024
You point to the top edge of the monitor, to a line of small type that she has not noticed. *Still. Front 0. Idle 790 W.* [Still = quiet; Leerlauf = idle]

### ch_11.025
"That is how much it costs to exist. Eight cards that do nothing. About a fan heater on a low setting." You nod toward the fan heater. "Less than that one there."

### ch_11.026
"Why is it worth pointing at that?" [lit. "Why does it pay, to point at it?"]

### ch_11.027
"Because a language model costs the same whether it thinks or not, and this here costs nothing until something happens, and then it costs exactly as much as the reach of this something. Wait."

### ch_11.028
You wait. She waits. She is good at that; you noticed that on Tuesday at the railing, before you knew her, in the way she read the hall.

### ch_11.029
Then the machines draw breath [lit. "fetch air"].

### ch_11.030
It is the fans. They come from nothing to something, a deep [tief = deep or low-pitched] exhaling, maybe half a second long, and on the monitor a fleck of color trembles at the eastern edge of the surface [Fläche; distinct from "Feld" = field], a wave that runs across a few hundred cells and dies away, and the line of type says *Front 14* and then *Front 3* and then *Still*, and the fans go down again, and the room is as it was.

### ch_11.031
"What was that?"

### ch_11.032
"An airplane."

### ch_11.033
She turns around and looks at you.

### ch_11.034
"On my windowsill upstairs there is a receiver. Thirty dollars. As big as a thumb. Every airplane within a radius of about a hundred and fifty miles transmits once per second where it is, how high, in which direction. That is what it hears. That is its only sense, apart from a clock and its own temperature. One of them has just crossed the edge of what the antenna can see, and it reached exactly that far in and stopped [ambiguous: "es" = the system, or the airplane ("Eins davon")]."

### ch_11.035
"It knows that they are airplanes."

### ch_11.036
"It does not know what an airplane is. It does not have the knot for it. It knows that every evening around seven something comes down out of its northeast that is not like the others, and that at nine something heavy climbs in this direction, and that at one o'clock at night there is a lot going on in its south, with things that come in low and do not leave again." You stop. "Freight. It does not know that either."

### ch_11.037
"How long has it been listening?"

### ch_11.038
"Since June. The fourteenth."

### ch_11.039
"On the company's cards."

### ch_11.040
"On the sandbox. It runs in the plan under my name [= it is booked/listed under my name]. Test and development."

### ch_11.041
"Does Dana know about it?" [lit. "Does Dana know notice?"]

### ch_11.042
"Dana knows that I use the sandbox."

### ch_11.043
"That is not the same."

### ch_11.044
"No."

### ch_11.045
To that she says nothing, and you are grateful to her. She turns back to the screen.

### ch_11.046
"Why is it *worth* pointing at? The breathing." [lit. "Why does it *pay*, to point at it?"]

### ch_11.047
"Because a lava lamp costs the same the whole night. This here costs nothing when the sky is empty, and something when the sky changes, and the something follows the sky. That was the first thing I had to know. Whether it is a very expensive lava lamp."

### ch_11.048
"And it isn't that." [lit. "And that it is not."]

### ch_11.049
"Not that, anyway."

#### notes ch_11
- "eine Farbfläche, so groß wie eine Tür" (ch_11.012): a door-sized color field on a desk monitor reads as physically odd; the intended image is unclear.
- "Windrad" (ch_11.013, .015): reads first as "wind turbine" for many readers; "Windrädchen" would make the toy pinwheel unambiguous.
- „Das sind seine Begriffe.“ (ch_11.015): opaque; unclear whose terms ("seine" = the system's? Kelvin's?) and what is meant.
- "Dann holen die Maschinen Luft." / "ein tiefes Ausatmen" (ch_11.029-.030): an inhale immediately described as an exhale; slightly mismatched image.
- "und es hat genau so weit hineingereicht und aufgehört" (ch_11.034): "es" can be read as the airplane rather than the system.
