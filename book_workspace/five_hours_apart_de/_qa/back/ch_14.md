### ch_14.001
## XIV. Forty-One, Once More

### ch_14.002
You do not drive home.

### ch_14.003
You take the Carpenter Freeway back into the city, with the heating switched on and the window closed and both hands on the steering wheel, and the skyline emerges ahead of you, as it always does, and you look at it anyway. At the office the barrier of the underground garage lifts for your ID badge. The security guard in the lobby is a different one and raises two fingers, and you raise two.

### ch_14.004
The elevator takes you up from the bottom of the underground garage, forty-five floors, and your ears do that thing. The floor is dark. As you come in, the motion detectors wake the lights ahead of you, one row after the other, so that you move through the office in a small wandering island of light, the opposite of Tuesday, when they [ambiguous: "sie" = the lights, the motion detectors, or unnamed people] put the office to sleep behind you. Your desk stands where you left it. On the windowsill the receiver blinks once per second.

### ch_14.005
You sit down, flip open the laptop and call up the surface [Fläche].

### ch_14.006
It is still. Front zero. Two knots at the eastern edge, turning against each other. At the very top of the log, under the line that says *still* at 19:15, there stands one more.

### ch_14.007
```
10-30 21:00:00  expects   N→NE heavy climbing   window 21:15–21:45
```
[erwartet = expects/expected; schwer steigend = heavy rising/climbing; Fenster = window]

### ch_14.008
You look out of the window. The city is sharp and cold and reaches all the way to its edges. The freeways are white toward you and red away from you. Far out behind Irving the airport glows in its matte amber, and the lights now come in from the south, in their patient row, each of them a person who is being picked up, or not.

### ch_14.009
At 21:52 a light lifts out of the amber, slowly, the way the heavies do, and climbs to the north and begins its long curve to the east, toward the Atlantic, toward a city that it will reach while it is still dark here. You watch it [lit. "look after it"] the whole way, until it is a star among stars and then no longer. Forty-five floors below you, in a room that nobody wanted, the fans have breathed once and gone down again. On the laptop a line has closed.

### ch_14.011
It knows that something heavy has climbed to the northeast, within its window. More it does not know. You too. [ambiguous: "Du auch." = "you too / you also" (i.e., you know that much too); after the negative, "neither do you" would normally be "Du auch nicht"]

### ch_14.012
It is a Friday. It is the last night of the skewed week [schräg = slanted/odd]. On Sunday at two it will be one again, and you will be in the room for it, and it too. Until then nothing is pending [lit. "nothing stands on"]. Up here the lights go out by themselves when you stop moving, and you do not stop, not for a good while yet.

#### notes ch_14
- "Ganz oben im Protokoll, unter der Zeile, die um 19:15 *still* sagt" (ch_14.006): "at the very top" yet "under" the 19:15 line; and in ch_12 one scrolls *up* to go back in time, so the newest line should be at the bottom.
- „Mehr weiß es nicht. Du auch.“ (ch_14.011): after a negative, "Du auch." reads as wrong or at least jarring; "Du auch nicht." would be the expected form if "neither do you" is meant.
- "als sie das Büro hinter dir schlafen gelegt haben" (ch_14.004): "sie" has no clear antecedent (lights? motion detectors?).
- "Die Stadt ist scharf und kalt und reicht bis an ihre Ränder." (ch_14.008): "reicht bis an ihre Ränder" is vague and reads oddly.
- "jedes von ihnen ein Mensch" (ch_14.008): each light "is a person" reads slightly odd; "steht für einen Menschen" or similar would be smoother.
