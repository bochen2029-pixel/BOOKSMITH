# Back-translation (de-DE → EN), unit: ch_09 — Half A, blind

### ch_09.001
## IX. Stemmons, Once More

### ch_09.002
The parking valet brings the car around. The night has not cooled down one bit. You get in, and the screen offers you what it always offers: HOME, the blue stripe, the smooth gray world, the careful dozen futures that the car imagines per second. You leave it at that [es dabei lassen = leave it at that / let it be]. You put both hands on the steering wheel, and you drive.

### ch_09.003
You also let the window down. The air comes in warm, the pavement is still giving off the heat of the day, and the sound of the tires comes in with it, and the hum of the freeway, and somewhere a siren that is not meant for you.

### ch_09.004
Stemmons, northbound. The same warehouses, the same levee, the same dark floodplain to the left of you, and the ball of the Reunion Tower in the mirror, growing smaller and shining on. You drive past the exit to the airport and this time leave it lying [= pass it by]. On the right a 737 with all its lights on comes down over Bachman Lake, so low and so close that you can read the airline on the tail, the last one [ambiguous: "die letzte" = the last plane (die 737) or the last airline (die Airline)] that is coming in from somewhere.

### ch_09.005
The dashboard shows 11:31. In London it is 4:31, almost morning, and she is sleeping, or almost, somewhere in the high-rise behind you, five hours ahead. On Sunday your (pl.) clocks [eure = your (pl.), i.e. the Dallas / US side] will be set back, and London will be six hours away again, where it belongs, and by then she is already home. None of it is sad, really. It is only true.

### ch_09.006
Far in the northwest, higher and slower than the traffic to Love Field, a light hangs in the dark and sinks toward the airport. Then another one behind it, and another: the same patient row that you sorted at seven from the forty-first floor, without once asking yourself who sits in it. Now you ask yourself. Someone on this plane is being waited for. Someone at those frosted-glass doors is holding up a phone with a name on it, the arm gets tired, switches arms. And someone on this plane comes through those doors, and no one is waiting, and tells himself [grammatically the elided subject could be "niemand": "and no one tells himself"] that it's fine. It will be fine. You hope that someone holds up a sign anyway.

### ch_09.007
Tomorrow, when someone asks you what you did tonight, you will say you gave a colleague [female, Kollegin] a ride from the airport [ambiguous: "eine Kollegin vom Flughafen mitgenommen" can also read "took along a colleague from the airport"]. It will be true, the way a list of notes [Noten = musical notes / sheet music; also "grades"] is true about a song [stimmen = be correct; also "be in tune"].

### ch_09.008
It is a Tuesday. It is the last week of October. The window is open, the night is warm, and you drive.

#### notes ch_09
- "und niemand wartet, und sagt sich, das sei schon in Ordnung": the elided subject of "sagt sich" grammatically attaches to "niemand" (no one waits and no one tells himself); repeating the subject would fix it.
- "der Arm wird müde, wechselt den Arm": "der Arm" becomes the subject of "wechselt", so the arm switches the arm; reads odd.
- "die letzte, die von irgendwo hereinkommt": nearest feminine antecedent is "die Airline", not the 737.
- "so wie eine Liste von Noten über ein Lied stimmt": "Noten" can be grades; "stimmen" doubles as "be in tune", which is a nice pun if intended.
- "sie schläft, oder fast" contrasts with ch_08 (she is awake at 11:31); presumably deliberate (the narrator's guess), not a translation issue.
