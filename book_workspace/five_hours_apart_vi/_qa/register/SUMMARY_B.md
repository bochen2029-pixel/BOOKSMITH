## Judgment

For most of its length, Half B reads as a book written in Vietnamese.

The dialogue is the strongest part. Iris's wryness comes through in natural particles ("cơ mà", "đâu có", "nhé"). The father sounds clipped and old ("như một lão ngốc"). The locked lines sit well in their sentences, and the Part Three rows read as a terse, consistent machine idiom.

The translation shows in two places:
- **The narrator's explanations in XI–XII.** English clause shape comes through here: post-posed relatives, "của việc", "mà ở đó", and pronouns stacked where Vietnamese would restructure.
- **About a dozen literal choices that trip over an idiom.** Two of them reverse the meaning: ch_10.013 and ch_12.040.

Neither problem is pervasive, and the fixes are short. With the two BLOCKs, the XIV echo repairs and the idiom-collision FIXes applied, a native reader would meet a plain, exact, dry voice. What foreignness remains in the technical passages fits an engineer who talks in specifications.
