## The three most serious problems
1. **VII.014 "bốn mươi bộ đèn neon đỏ" (BLOCK).** It reads as "forty sets of lights", because "bộ đèn" means a light fixture. Fix: "một khối đèn neon đỏ cao bốn mươi bộ".
2. **V.025 "sáng ở đầu gần như một chiếc đèn lồng" (BLOCK).** "gần như" is read as "almost", so "the near end" is lost and "almost like a lantern" is added. Fix: "ở đầu bên này, như…".
3. **VI.035 "các cô" for "you in London" (FIX, twice).** It says "you ladies are ahead of us". The scene's own pair elsewhere is "bên cô / bên tôi" (.033, .084).

Two meaning slips sit just below these:
- **VII.009 "Có bao giờ anh rời đi không?"** asks "Have you ever left?". The English is "Would you ever leave?", which his piano answer needs.
- **III.013 "bố sẽ chẳng thấy phiền gì"** turns the past "wouldn't have minded" into a future.

## Judgment
The dialogue is the strongest part and reads as Vietnamese. Iris's wit survives ("Dana xung phong hộ tôi", "cảm ơn anh đã bị xung phong hộ", "Thế à.", "Tôi đâu dám cãi lời Dana"). The particles fall where a speaker would put them ("cơ mà", "nhỉ", "đâu"), the father is terse, the locked lines sit naturally, and the dash punctuation is clean throughout.

The narration is where the English shows. Roughly every second or third paragraph of the dense chapters (III, VI) has a "với" construction, a calqued relative clause, an object "nó", or an aside in English word order. A Vietnamese reader rarely stumbles on these, but together they mark the prose as translated. Only the two BLOCKs actually mislead.

With those fixed and the FIX round applied, Half A would read as a careful, warm Vietnamese literary translation: a book a reader in Hanoi or Saigon accepts without friction, though not yet one they would take for a Vietnamese original. The remaining gap is the "với" and relative-clause habit in the narration, not vocabulary or register.
