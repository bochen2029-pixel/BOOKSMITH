# Back-translation (vi-VN → English), blind, half A: ch_09

### ch_09.001
## IX. Stemmons, once more

### ch_09.002
The parking attendant brings the car around. The night has not eased [dịu: grown milder / cooled] at all. He gets in, and the screen offers him what it always offers: HOME, the green [xanh] band, the smooth gray world, the dozen careful futures per second that the car imagines. He leaves it alone. He puts both hands on the wheel, and he drives.

### ch_09.003
He lowers the window as well [or: further; "nữa"]. The air rushes in warm, the road surface still giving back the day's heat, and the sound of the tires rushes in with it, then the hum of the freeway, and somewhere a siren that is not meant for him.

### ch_09.004
Stemmons, northbound. The same warehouses, the same levee, the same pitch-dark floodway on the left, and the ball of Reunion Tower in the mirror, getting smaller, still lit. He passes the airport exit and this time lets it drift by. On the right, a 737 comes down over Bachman Lake with all its lights on, so low and close that he can read the airline's name on the tail, the last flight coming back from somewhere.

### ch_09.005
The dashboard clock reads 11:31. In London it is 4:31, nearly morning, and she is asleep, or almost, somewhere in the tower behind him, five hours ahead. This Sunday the clocks on his side will go back, and London will be six hours away again, in its proper place, and by then she will have gone home. There is nothing sad in that, strictly speaking. It is just the truth.

### ch_09.006
Far off to the northwest, higher and slower than the stream of Love Field planes, a light hangs in the dark, sinking little by little toward the airport. Then another behind it, then one more: the same patient row he sorted from the forty-first floor at seven o'clock without once asking himself who was up there. Now he does ask himself. Someone on that plane is being waited for. Someone in front of those frosted glass doors is holding up a phone with a name on it, arm tired, switching hands. And someone on that plane will walk through those doors with nobody waiting, telling themselves that it's fine. It will be fine. He hopes that someone holds up a sign anyway.

### ch_09.007
Tomorrow, if anyone asks what he did tonight, he will say he gave a colleague a ride back from the airport. That will be true, the way a row of musical notes is true about a song.

### ch_09.008
It is a Tuesday. It is the last week of October. The window is down, the night is warm, and he is driving.

#### notes ch_09
- "Anh hạ cửa kính xuống nữa": "nữa" reads either as "as well" or as "further", which suggests the window was already partly down; neither is set up earlier.
- "Đêm chẳng dịu đi chút nào": "dịu" (to soften / grow mild) is a little vague; a reader takes it as "the night has not cooled off".
- ch_09.005 has him picture her asleep ("cô đang ngủ, hoặc gần như thế") while ch_08 shows her awake at the same 11:31; this reads as deliberate irony, not an error.
