### ch_07.001
## VII. Elm Street

### ch_07.002
Downtown at a quarter past eleven on a Tuesday night is a stage after the show. The streets are warm and almost deserted, the traffic lights keep changing color for nobody. A man sprays water to wash down a restaurant's patio, and the smell of wet concrete crosses the street to meet him. A security guard in a building lobby lifts two fingers off the desk as he walks past, and he lifts two fingers back.

### ch_07.003
She walks with the coat draped over her arm, still, as if she has carried it so long she has forgotten it is there. She walks the way she walked through the doors: reading everything.

### ch_07.004
—Is it always this empty here?

### ch_07.005

### ch_07.006
—That's hardly an answer.

### ch_07.007
—It's the only answer people here ever give.

### ch_07.008
A tram [tàu điện: electric train / tram] comes along Pacific Avenue as the two of them cross it, two cars, lit the way a kitchen is lit at three in the morning, not a single person on board, then glides off toward the West End.

### ch_07.009

### ch_07.010

### ch_07.011
—Meaning what?

### ch_07.012

### ch_07.013
She laughs, and the laugh flies up the empty street and does not come back.

### ch_07.014
Then he is already right underneath it. From the forty-eighth floor, it was a toy, a thing he could pick up. From the street, it is forty feet of red neon on a steel drilling rig, turning so slowly you have to watch a while to be sure, two horses with their backs to each other so that whichever way he stands there is one charging toward him. The red covers everything: the sidewalk, the glass windows across the street, her face as she tips her head back to look.

### ch_07.015

### ch_07.016

### ch_07.017

### ch_07.018

### ch_07.019

### ch_07.020

### ch_07.021

### ch_07.022

### ch_07.023

### ch_07.024
