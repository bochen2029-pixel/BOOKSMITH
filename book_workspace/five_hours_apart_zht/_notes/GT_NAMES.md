# GT_NAMES — Proper nouns in "Five Hours Apart" (ground-truth lane GT-NAMES)

Prepared 2026-10-06 for the zh-Hant-TW translation (and the zh-Hans derivative). Read-only research lane; the moderator rules.

**Access notes (affects evidence quality).** In this sandbox the egress proxy blocked every direct dictionary/encyclopedia host: wikidata.org, zh.wikipedia.org (all variants), wikiwand.com, the zhwiki mirror, terms.naer.edu.tw (page fetch), moedict.tw (page fetch), cna.com.tw and visitdallas.com (page fetch). Evidence therefore comes from WebSearch result snippets that quote those sites (NAER, 萌典, zh.wikipedia, 中央社, Visit Dallas 中文版, 新华网, ctrip, Costco Taiwan, etc.). Where a form is cited from a snippet rather than a fetched page it is marked "(snippet)". Confidence: H = multiple independent or authoritative sources; M = one good source or strong convention; L = reasoned proposal, no direct evidence found.

**Conventions used below.** TW = Taiwan publishing/press practice; CN = mainland practice (新华社《英语姓名译名手册》/《世界人名翻译大辞典》 conventions where known). "Latin pair at first use" = `中文（Latin）` once, then Chinese alone. For fictional people the Taiwan practice is a transliteration that reads as a name (feminine endings 絲/娜 for women); the mainland practice is the Xinhua handbook form.

**Cross-cutting rulings to request from the moderator (my recommendations):**
1. **Machine text stays Latin.** The tape rows, the Part Three log, the car screen (HOME), the garage level (P4), the airport board (LONDON HEATHROW / DELAYED / LANDED / AT GATE), airport and runway codes (DFW, LHR, DAL, 27L, 17/18), UTC, and the label strip (KELVIN) are things the characters *see*; keep them in Latin capitals exactly as set. Prose and dialogue carry the Chinese forms.
2. **The phone sign.** "IRIS WREN" typed on a phone in a Dallas terminal is an object in the world; keep it as `IRIS WREN` in Latin inside the prose line ("你打開空白備忘錄，輸入 IRIS WREN，把字放到最大"), and have her read "her own name" in Chinese prose. This also keeps the Part Three row `IRIS "That's me."` consistent.
3. **"Who's Kelvin?"** The door label is `KELVIN`; her question and his answer use the locale's Kelvin form (TW 克耳文 / CN 开尔文). Part Three's row `field name KELVIN from a label strip, a door, P4` stays Latin.
4. **Streets translate, towns transliterate, districts mostly translate** (evidence below): 榆樹街 / 商業街 / 太平洋大道 translate; 達拉斯 / 尤利斯 / 歐文 / 阿靈頓 transliterate; 西區 / 設計區 translate; Deep Ellum and Las Colinas are the two districts where Chinese-language media themselves either keep Latin or use unstable transliterations: keep Latin with a Latin pair.
