\## One. Forty-One

**002**

**003**

**004**

**005**

For a week, it has passed forty-nine times out of fifty and failed the fiftieth, and nobody can find the reason, which means there is a reason, only nobody has found it yet. **006**

**007**

**008**

The one nobody pays me to answer is whether anything is like anything to it. When it gets something wrong, does that count as anything? When the elevator goes down, do its ears block up?"

**056**
"Do you think they do?" [Do you think so?]

**057**
"Not the ones we build. Not yet. But I don't think it's the material. I think it's the shape. I think if you get the processing right, the kind of thing it does to itself right, then it will be there, in silicon, or in meat, or in a sheet of anything at all, and it won't care what it's running on." You hear your own voice and lower it. "And you'll never know. That's the part. You could build it, and sit across a table from it, and never know."

**058**
"Does it make a difference? If you can't tell."

**059**
"It does to it."

**060**
She says nothing for a while, and you have time to hear what you have just done, which is give a speech at a dinner table to a woman who has not slept in twenty-four hours.

**061**
"Sorry. That's the speech I don't give anyone."

**062**
"I noticed you had it ready."

**063**
"Since about 2019."

**064**
She laughs, not the polite kind. And there is a pleasure in this, not the fix, not the view, and not the thing you noticed at the door and then looked away from. It is the pleasure of handing something you care about to a person and watching them hold it properly. You had forgotten it was on the list.

**065**
"What would you have been doing tonight?" she asks. "If Dana hadn't texted."

**066**
"Honestly?"

**067**
"Honestly."

**068**
"I would fly to London."

**069**
She waits.

**070**
"In a simulator. On the computer at home. It's what I have instead of television. Tonight was going to be the approach into Heathrow."

**071**
"You were going to land at Heathrow tonight."

**072**
"Two-seven left. Usually there's a west wind. You come in over the city—"

**073**
"I know how it comes in. I've come in a hundred times."

**074**
"From inside."

**075**
"From inside. What you see is a seatback and a man's elbow."

**076**
"From the front, it's the best ten minutes there is. Seven hours of nothing, and then the whole city comes up out of the dark, and you have ten minutes not to mess it up."

**077**
"But why the landing? Out of the whole trip."

**078**
You think about it, because she asks like someone who wants the real answer. "Cruise flies itself. It doesn't need anyone. Landing is the only part where it makes a difference whether there's anyone in the seat."

**079**
She looks at you for a moment, a little longer than the line needs.

**080**
"So you've seen my city from the front a hundred times," she says, "and never once been in it."

**081**
"And you've been in it twelve years, and never once seen it from the front."

**082**
After that, neither of you says anything for a while, and it isn't awkward, which surprises you. Below the window, a tram slides along Pacific Avenue, lit, empty. To the west, the amber over the airport hasn't changed. It won't.

**083**
"In my body it's four in the morning," she says after a while. "You know the one thing I like about jet lag? For a day or two you aren't anywhere. You're in between."

**084**
"Everyone is, this week. Your clocks have changed, ours haven't."

**085**
"Five hours instead of six."

**086**
"Until Sunday."

**087**
"Until Sunday," she agrees, and looks out at the city again, and that is all either of you says about it.

**088**
The bill comes, and you hand over the company card as instructed. By the time the waiter brings the card back, it is past eleven here, which is past four in her body, and it is starting to show. She is still lit, but lit like a phone with two percent battery left.

**089**
"I can't sleep," she says. "Not for at least an hour. I know how this goes." She looks down out of the window. "Could we see the horse? From underneath?"

## ch_07

**001**
\## Seven. Elm Street

**002**
Downtown at a quarter past eleven on a Tuesday night is a stage after the show. The streets are warm, almost empty, the traffic lights cycling round and round for no one. A man is hosing down a patio, and the smell of wet concrete crosses the street to meet you. As you pass, a guard in a lobby lifts two fingers off his desk, and you lift two back.

**003**
She walks with the coat still over her arm, as if she has carried it so long she has forgotten it exists. She walks the way she came through that door: reading everything.

**004**
"Is it always this empty here?"

**005**
"At night. In the day it's hotter."

**006**
"That's not an answer."

**007**
"It's the only answer people here give."

**008**
As you cross Pacific Avenue, a tram comes along the street, two cars, lit like a kitchen at three in the morning, not a soul on board, sliding away toward the West End [the west district].

**009**
"Would you leave?" she asks. "Dallas."

**010**
"I think about it the way I think about learning the piano."

**011**
"Meaning?"

**012**
"With longing. Once every few years."

**013**
She laughs, and the laugh goes out along the empty street and does not come back.

**014**
And then you are under it. From the forty-eighth floor it is a toy, something you could pick up. From the street it is forty feet of red neon on a steel frame, turning so slowly you have to watch for a while to be sure, two horses back to back, so that whichever way you face, one is coming toward you. The red gets on everything: the sidewalk, the windows across the street, her face as she tilts it up to look.

**015**
"It's bigger from underneath."

**016**
"Most things are."

**017**
"It's turning."

**018**
"It has always been turning."

**019**
She watches it turn most of the way round. "I'd use it too," she says. "If I were the one flying in. I'd fly toward it."

**020**
Back at the hotel, the door is the revolving kind, and the lobby behind it is all marble and quiet. She turns and holds out her hand, and you take it, a handshake of exactly the right length.

**021**
"Seven," she says.

**022**
"Seven."

**023**
"Thank you," she says. "For the sign."

**024**
The door turns and takes her, and turns once more, empty. All night you watched doors open and close for people you didn't know. This time you know who is behind the door.

\## Eight. Four in the Morning

**002**
Upstairs, in a room on the twenty-second floor with the curtains open, Iris Wren is not asleep.

**003**
She has taken off her shoes and nothing else. The suitcase stands against the wall, right where the bellhop set it down, leaning on that wheel. The coat is on the chair. The bed is still made, and she sits at the foot of it, hands on her knees, looking at the city.

**004**
From here, the horse is almost at eye level, a few blocks away, turning. A tram goes along Pacific Avenue, two cars, lit, empty, and the buildings take it away. The freeway is white on one side and red on the other. Far off, where he pointed, the sky over the airport is a dull, unchanging amber.

**005**

**006**

**007**
By her body's time it is 4:31, by the clock on the nightstand it is 11:31, and she is still there, the horse turning, the city spread out to its edge, waiting for nothing, and with nothing due to come [nothing that ought to come].

## ch_09

**001**
\## Nine. Stemmons, Again

**002**
The valet brings the car around. The night has not cooled at all. You get in, and the screen gives you what it always gives you: "Home", the blue ribbon, the smooth grey world, the car's dozen or so careful futures a second. You leave it sitting there. You put both hands on the wheel, and you drive.

**003**
You roll the window down too. The air that comes in is warm, the road still giving back the day's heat, and the sound of the tires comes in with it, and the hum of the freeway, and somewhere a siren that is not for you.

**004**
Stemmons, north. The same warehouses, the same levee, the same black floodway on the left, and the ball of Reunion Tower in the mirror, smaller and smaller, still lit. You pass the exit for the airport, and this time you let it go by. On the right, a 737 comes down over Bachman Lake, every light on, so low and so close you can read which airline is on the tail, the last one in from somewhere.

**005**
The dashboard says 11:31. London is 4:31, almost morning, and she is asleep, or nearly, somewhere in that tower behind you, five hours ahead of you. On Sunday the clocks on your side go back, and London will be six hours away again, where it belongs, and by then she will be home. None of this counts as sad. It's just true.

**006**
Far to the northwest, higher and slower than the Love Field flights, a point of light hangs in the dark, sinking toward the airport. Then another behind it, and another: the same patient line you picked out from the forty-first floor at seven, without once thinking about who was up there. Now you do. Someone on that plane is being waited for. Someone in front of those frosted glass doors is holding up a phone with a name on it, arm getting sore, switching hands. And someone on that plane will come out through those doors with no one waiting, telling themselves it's fine. It will be fine. You hope someone holds up a sign anyway. [You wish there were still someone holding up a sign.]

**007**
Tomorrow, if anyone asks what you did tonight, you'll say you picked up a colleague from the airport. That will be true, the way a list of notes is true of a song.

**008**
It is a Tuesday. It is the last week of October. The window is open, the night is warm, and you are driving.

## Reader's notes

Places where I had to guess, where a line read unclear or wrong to me as a reader, where a word changed between chapters, or where a joke did not land. Block ids are unit/block.

**Guesses and ambiguous readings**

- front/002 — 假設 as a noun on the subtitle ("a hypothesis / a supposition") and 假設 as the verb that opens ch_01, ch_02, ch_03, ch_04 ("Suppose…") are one word in Chinese. I rendered the noun as "hypothesis"; a reader hears the chapter openers as the same word, so the subtitle reads as "A 'suppose', in three parts". If the English keeps them as two different words, that link is a Chinese-only gain; if the English has one word for both, it is preserved.
- front/003 — The row is opaque at this point: 房間 (room), 預期 (expected), 重型機 (heavy), 海上 (at sea), 未閉 (unclosed). I could not tell what the columns are. 未閉 reads literally as "not closed"; "open" would be the natural English if that is a status field. 海上 could also be "over the sea" (an aircraft position) rather than "at sea". I copied the time token and the date token as given.
- ch_01/006 and ch_05/019 — 程序 is the Taiwan word for an operating-system process, so "two processes" is the first reading; but a general reader also hears "two procedures". Consistent in both places.
- ch_01/011 — 中城區 read as a place name ("the Mid-Cities"), but the characters just say "mid-city district"; a reader who does not know the area takes it as a description, not a name. 達福機場 is a transliteration that a reader has to decode as DFW; it is not self-evident the first time (ch_01/011), only after "Terminal D" in ch_02/011.
- ch_02/003 — 商業街: the first reading for a Taiwanese reader is the common noun "the shopping street / a commercial street", not a street named Commerce. The sentence still works either way, but as a name it is invisible.
- ch_02/004 — 老木蘭大樓: 木蘭 alone evokes 花木蘭 (Mulan) before 木蘭花 (magnolia). "The old Mulan Building" is a possible first reading. 飛馬 reads as Pegasus (the constellation name) first and "flying horse" second; I kept "Pegasus" in ch_02/004, ch_06/004, ch_06/006 and ch_06/009, and "the horse" wherever the Chinese says 那匹馬.
- ch_02/009 — 駕駛盤 is a generic "steering/control wheel"; a reader pictures a car steering wheel clamped to a desk until 油門桿組 (a throttle lever set) and 模擬器 arrive in the next sentence. If the English says "yoke", the Chinese is less specific.
- ch_02/010 — 黛娜 could be Dana, Dinah or Dena; I used Dana throughout.
- ch_02/012 — 她的是好的那一種 ("Hers are the good kind"): the antecedent is unstated. I took it as her documents (from "a name at the top of a document"); on first reading it is briefly unclear what "hers" is.
- ch_02/015 — 我去 as a two-character dictated reply reads "I'll go"; a younger reader will flicker on the internet exclamation 我去 ("oh no!"), mostly a mainland usage, but context settles it.
- ch_03/001 — 國際大道 is "International Avenue/Boulevard"; the Chinese does not say "Parkway".
- ch_03/003, ch_06/082, ch_07/008, ch_08/004 — 電車 is used both for the vehicle that "runs inside security" at the airport and for the two-car vehicle along Pacific Avenue downtown. A reader takes both as the same kind of tram/light rail, which may or may not be what the English has (an airport people-mover vs a city train).
- ch_04/001 — 門 is unmarked for number: "The Door" or "The Doors". The arrivals doors are "a pair" (ch_03/008), but ch_07/024 ends on a single revolving door, so the title could be read as pointing to either.
- ch_04/013 — 差不多 is one word; "Roughly." is my one-word rendering, "About that." is the natural one.
- ch_05/016 — 「而且還很有信心。」 has no subject. First reading: the model is wrong *and confident about it* (the joke being that confident errors are the interesting ones, which 017 confirms). Second reading: "and you say so with great confidence", a tease aimed at "you". The ambiguity is probably intended but the first reading is the one that makes 017 land.
- ch_05/029 — 「是嗎。」 ends in a full stop, not a question mark; I read it as the flat "Did she." / "Is that so."
- ch_06/027 — 「那你來。」 is terse: "Then you (do it)", "Your turn", "Then you have a go". I rendered "So, your turn."
- ch_06/055 — 「有沒有任何東西對它來說像任何東西」 is hard going in Chinese; a reader reaches "whether anything is like anything to it" only on a second pass. It is the one line in the dinner conversation that reads as translated philosophy rather than speech. 「電梯往下的時候，它的耳朵會不會塞住？」 lands (it pays off ch_01/014 and ch_06/002).
- ch_06/056 — 「你覺得會嗎？」 — "Do you think they do (block up)?" / "Do you think so?" The 會 points back to 會不會塞住, so I kept the ears as the referent.
- ch_06/057 — 「在一片什麼都行的東西裡」 — "in a sheet [slice] of anything at all": the measure word 片 makes the thing flat; a reader pauses on it.
- ch_06/068 — 「我會飛去倫敦。」 is tenseless: "I'd fly to London" / "I'd be flying to London" / "I'd have flown to London". Any of the three; the joke (she waits; he means a simulator) lands in all of them.
- ch_07/008 — 西區: "the West End" if a reader knows it is a district name; otherwise "the west side". Not marked as a name in Chinese.
- ch_07/012 — 嚮往 is "to long for / yearn for"; "With longing." is my rendering; "Fondly." or "Wistfully." are neighbours.
- ch_08/007 — 也沒有什麼該來: "and nothing is due to come" / "nothing that ought to come". The phrase 沒有什麼該來 is the Part Two title in the heading list, so a reader will hear this line as the hinge.
- ch_09/006 — 「你希望還是有人舉起牌子。」 — first reading: "You hope someone holds up a sign anyway (even for the one nobody is waiting for)". Second reading: "You wish there were still someone holding up a sign." The 還是 carries both "nevertheless" and "still".

**Lines that read unclear or wrong to me as a reader**

- ch_01/010 — 高速公路朝你的是白，離你的是紅: literally "the freeways: toward you white, away from you red". A reader works out headlights/taillights, but the line is compressed to the point of a riddle on first reading.
- ch_02/004 — 以這種或那種形式 ("in one form or another") reads as an English idiom carried over; natural enough, but it is the one phrase in the paragraph that sounds translated.
- ch_02/011 — 她的接送車放棄了 ("her pickup car gave up"): a car "giving up" is odd in Chinese; the reader infers the driver/service cancelled.
- ch_03/013 — 喇叭播著塔台 ("the loudspeaker played the tower"): without knowing it is tower radio, the phrase is a small puzzle. 他晚上不開車了。他說是他的眼睛。 reads cleanly and lands.
- ch_04/003 — 也就是她寫東西的方式 ("which is the way she writes"): on its own the equation of a walk with a writing style is abrupt; it depends on the reader having kept ch_02/012 in mind.
- ch_05/022 — 「久了，沒有就睡不著。」 is very clipped; a reader fills in "the noise". Fine as speech.
- ch_06/022 — 「是有點什麼。我不確定是不是那個。」 — the two 什麼/那個 are deliberately vague; the reader has to supply "loneliness" from 017. Works, but only just.
- ch_06/088 — 她還是亮著的，但亮著，像一支電量剩百分之二的手機: the repeated 亮著 reads as a stumble in Chinese ("still lit, but lit, like…"); the image is clear, the syntax is not.
- ch_08/004 — 他指過的地方 ("where he pointed"): in ch_06 he never visibly points at the airport; the narration in ch_06/003 names the amber in the west without a gesture. A reader notices the gap only if looking for it.
- ch_08/007 — 以她身體的時間是 4:31 / 以床頭櫃上的時鐘是 11:31: digits with a leading space before them; the rest of Part One writes clock times in words (六點五十二分, 九點零四分) except the phone's 7:06 (ch_02/010), the board's 8:14 (ch_03/005) and the dashboard's 11:31 (ch_09/005).
