### ch_08.001  [h2]

## VIII. Four in the Morning

### ch_08.002  [para]

Upstairs, in a room on the twenty-second floor with the curtains open, Iris Wren does not sleep.

### ch_08.003  [para]

She has taken off her shoes and nothing else. The suitcase stands against the wall where the bellman left it, listing on its wheel. The coat is on the chair. The bed is still made, and she is sitting on the end of it with her hands in her lap, looking at the city.

### ch_08.004  [para]

From here the horse is nearly at eye level, a few blocks off, turning. A train goes along Pacific, two cars, lit and empty, and the buildings take it. The freeway runs white one way and red the other. Far out, where he pointed, the sky over the airport is a dull amber that doesn't change.

### ch_08.005  [para]

Her phone is face down on the nightstand. She doesn't turn it over.

### ch_08.006  [para]

The horse goes round. She watches it make a turn, and another, and if she is counting them the number doesn't matter. Once she looks at the coat on the chair, and once at the suitcase, and once at her own hands, and then back at the city. She doesn't get up. She doesn't try to sleep. She knows how this goes.

### ch_08.007  [para]

At 4:31 by her body and 11:31 by the clock on the nightstand she is still there, with the horse going round and the city laid out to its edges, and she is not waiting for anything, and nothing is due.
