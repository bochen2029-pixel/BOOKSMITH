# Worker pack: ch_09

### ch_09.001  [h2]

## IX. Stemmons, Again

### ch_09.002  [para]

The valet brings the car around. The night hasn't cooled at all. You get in, and the screen offers you what it always offers: HOME, the blue ribbon, the smooth gray world, the car's careful dozen futures a second. You leave it there. You put both hands on the wheel, and you drive.

### ch_09.003  [para]

You lower the window, too. The air comes in warm, the pavement still giving back the day's heat, and the sound of the tires comes in with it, and the hum of the freeway, and somewhere a siren that isn't for you.

### ch_09.004  [para]

Stemmons, north. The same warehouses, the same levee, the same dark floodway on your left, and Reunion's globe in the mirror, getting smaller, still glowing. You pass the exit for the airport and this time let it go by. Off to the right, a 737 comes down over Bachman Lake with everything lit, so low and so close that you can read the airline off its tail, the last one in from somewhere.

### ch_09.005  [para]

The dashboard says 11:31. In London it's 4:31, almost morning, and she's asleep, or close to it, somewhere in the tower behind you, five hours ahead. On Sunday your clocks go back, and London will be six hours away again, where it belongs, and by then she'll be home. None of that is sad, exactly. It's only true.

### ch_09.006  [para]

Far off to the northwest, higher and slower than the Love Field traffic, a light hangs in the dark, sinking toward the airport. Then another behind it, and another: the same patient line you sorted from the forty-first floor at seven o'clock without once wondering who was on them. You wonder now. Someone on that plane is being waited for. Someone at those frosted doors is holding up a phone with a name on it, arm getting tired, switching arms. And someone on that plane is coming through those doors with nobody waiting, telling themselves it's fine. It will be fine. You hope someone holds up a sign anyway.

### ch_09.007  [para]

Tomorrow, if anyone asks what you did tonight, you'll say you gave a colleague a ride from the airport. It will be true, the way a list of notes is true about a song.

### ch_09.008  [para]

It's a Tuesday. It's the last week of October. The window is down, the night is warm, and you are driving.

## Forbidden in prose (FAIL unless marked WARN)

- `[喔耶啦囉齁欸嘛哦]」`  FAIL  sentence-final particles: the author's register has none; the dryness is the voice
- `[喔耶啦囉齁欸]。`  FAIL  sentence-final particles in narration
- `值得注意的是|總而言之|綜上所述|不得不說|眾所周知|在這個瞬間|在此刻|毫無疑問地`  FAIL  AI tells and essay furniture; the book never explains itself
- `的的`  FAIL  a doubled 的 is a typo
- `妳`  FAIL  the book is in one 你 for everyone; 妳 would gender what the source does not
- `牠`  FAIL  the field is 它, never 牠
- `令人`  WARN  "令人(動容|不安|…)" is sentiment the source refuses; translate what happened, not what to feel

- `彷彿在訴說|似乎在訴說|訴說著`  WARN  translationese personification
- `進行了?[一]?[個次番]?`  WARN  進行 + noun is bureaucratic; use the verb
- `[^，。！？：；、「」『』（）《》……—— \n*>`\w\d]{0}(?:在這裡|在那裡)，`  WARN  place adverbials with a comma after are translationese
