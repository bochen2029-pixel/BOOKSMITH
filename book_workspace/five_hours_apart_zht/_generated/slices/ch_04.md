# Worker pack: ch_04

Segments (translate block for block; keep the count exactly):

### ch_04.001  [h2]

## IV. The Doors

### ch_04.002  [para]

Say the doors open on her at four minutes past nine.

### ch_04.003  [para]

You don't recognize her. There's nothing to recognize; a postage stamp isn't a face. What you recognize is the way she comes through, which is the way she writes. She isn't looking for anyone. She's looking at everything, reading the hall left to right as though it were a page she means to read properly. She has a wool coat folded over one arm, a London coat, wrong for here, and she's pulling a suitcase that lists to one side on a broken wheel.

### ch_04.004  [para]

You register that she is beautiful the way your ears registered the descent from the forty-first floor: not as a thought, just as a change your body notices before you've decided anything about it. Then you look at the suitcase.

### ch_04.005  [para]

Her eyes find the phone. She comes to a stop in front of you, reads her own name, and looks up.

### ch_04.006  [dialogue]

"That's me," she says. "I've never had a sign before."

### ch_04.007  [dialogue]

"It's a phone."

### ch_04.008  [dialogue]

"It's a sign," she says, mildly, as if correcting a definition in a draft, and you decide she's right.

### ch_04.009  [para]

You tell her your name. She says it back carefully, like someone who intends to remember it. Her accent is something northern that twelve years of London have flattened without quite erasing, and she pronounces your name as though it has one more syllable than you've been giving it.

### ch_04.010  [dialogue]

"Dana said you volunteered."

### ch_04.011  [dialogue]

"Dana volunteered me."

### ch_04.012  [dialogue]

"Then thank you for being volunteered." A smile, there and gone. "And I'm so sorry. You must have been here an hour."

### ch_04.013  [dialogue]

"About that."

### ch_04.014  [dialogue]

"That's awful."

### ch_04.015  [dialogue]

"It wasn't," you say, and you mean it, and you can't explain why without sounding strange, so you don't.

### ch_04.016  [para]

You reach for the suitcase. She says it's fine, it's broken, it's embarrassing. You say you've got it. There's one more round of the old ritual in which two strangers decline and accept the same favor, and then she lets you, and the two of you walk out across the skybridge to the garage, the broken wheel ticking behind you over every seam in the floor.

### ch_04.017  [para]

The garage is open to the night, and the air moving through it is seventy-one degrees and smells of jet fuel and cut grass. She stops walking.

### ch_04.018  [dialogue]

"It's warm," she says, as if reporting a fault. "It's the end of October."

### ch_04.019  [dialogue]

"It's the end of October in Texas."

### ch_04.020  [para]

She looks down at the coat over her arm like it has personally let her down.

## Forbidden in prose (FAIL unless marked WARN)

- `[喔耶啦囉齁欸]。`  FAIL  sentence-final particles in narration

- `的的`  FAIL  a doubled 的 is a typo
- `妳`  FAIL  the book is in one 你 for everyone; 妳 would gender what the source does not
- `牠`  FAIL  the field is 它, never 牠

- `進行了?[一]?[個次番]?`  WARN  進行 + noun is bureaucratic; use the verb
