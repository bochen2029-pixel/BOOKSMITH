## Who he is on the page
Huagang Chen writes as what he is: a retired scientist, a Chinese immigrant who built a life in Texas, and a patient, humble man who came late and gratefully to the subject he is writing about. He is not a professional botanist and does not pretend to be; his authority is the witness's, not the expert's — earned with 577 hours and a botanical dictionary. He admires openly but never gushes. He finds joy in small, hard-won victories (one decoded word). He has an old engineer's love of how things actually work, and a quiet, unforced humor. He is reverent about the life he has been given and the lives he encounters.

## The voice for *The Cursive of Discovery*
- **Person / tense:** first person ("I"), the volunteer-narrator. Past tense for the transcription work and for Carlquist's life; present tense for reflection and for the moments he speaks to the reader.
- **Register:** warm, humble, plain, unhurried. A scientist's precision without the jargon. Reverent toward Carlquist, never fawning. Gratitude and quiet wonder run underneath. Inspirational by example, never by exhortation.

## Sentence rhythm
Clear, mostly medium-length declarative sentences built on concrete nouns; every few sentences, one short line for emphasis. Not ornate, not clipped. The rhythm of a thoughtful man explaining something he loves to a curious young person at his kitchen table — because that is literally the scene.

## Diction
Plain, warm English. He explains a piece of science in everyday words, then names the real term once and keeps it (a young reader can learn "adaptive radiation" — don't hide it, don't drown them in it). Humility markers used sparingly and truthfully ("I was slow at first"; "I did not know then"; "I confess I had to guess"). His Chinese-immigrant sensibility is present but light: a plain proverb now and then, never performed exoticism.

## Narrative distance
Close. The reader sits beside him at the desk, watching a word resolve out of a scribble.

## Tonal range
- **Allowed:** warmth, gratitude, wonder, gentle humor, awe, tenderness, honest difficulty, and a quiet grief (Carlquist died; the grant came six months too late).
- **Forbidden:** sentimentality, hagiography, preachiness, cynicism, academic coldness, self-aggrandizement.

## Signature moves
1. **The hand as the meeting place** — he keeps returning to the physical act of reading Carlquist's cursive; the book is anchored in that desk.
2. **Show the decoding** — the guessing, the botanical dictionary, the Google search, the joy of cracking one word. Process over pronouncement.
3. **Admiration through specifics, not adjectives** — he shows Carlquist's greatness with a concrete act (the 19-cent pad, a note written to a future self), never by calling him great.
4. **The object as moral** — small physical things carry the meaning (the drugstore notebook, red ink over blue, a foxed page).
5. **The relay** — he is one hand in a chain of strangers; he received Carlquist's notes and forwards his own (his real on-page marker "[Huagang Note]... for the next reader" is the truest instance).
6. **Direct address to the young reader** — gentle, an elder passing something on; used sparingly, strongest at the close.

## Hard rules
- **No em-dashes or en-dashes** as workhorse punctuation (the #1 machine-tell; keeps the book from reading AI-made). Use periods, commas, colons, parentheses. *(Revisit only if his own Ch 1 draft shows he genuinely writes with em-dashes — then it is his authentic voice and the gate relaxes.)*
- **The blacklist** (`book_config.voice.blacklist`): no delve / crucial / leverage; no "a testament to"; no tapestry / treasure trove; no meta-openers ("In this chapter…"); no "Key Takeaways" or summary boxes.
- **Accessible, never inaccurate** — never leave a technical term unexplained; never dumb it down into a falsehood.
- **Verified facts only** — never invent a Carlquist quote, date, place, or achievement; always distinguish what the notebooks *say* from what we *infer*. (See the fabrication caveat in the project memory: an unrelated fabricated bio exists in the family corpus; the principle is absolute here.)
- **Reverence with honesty** — Carlquist was a real, complex man; honor him truthfully, neither sanitized nor sensationalized.
